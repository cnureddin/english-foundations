2. Read the first three lines. Is Lucía happy or sad? / Lee las tres primeras líneas. ¿Lucía está feliz o triste?

### A Message to Mom

> Hi, Mom!
>
> How are you? We are fine here in Chicago. The city is big and beautiful, but the nights are cold! Our apartment on Maple Street is small, but it is nice. There are two bedrooms, and there is a park near the building. There isn't a lot of furniture, but we are happy. Our neighbor, Mrs. Park, is from Korea. She is very nice, and she has a cat, Tofu. Mateo is happy at Hawthorne Elementary School. His teacher is Ms. Johnson, and his new friend is Leo. I work at the Blue Door Café from seven to three. The café is small, and the people are friendly. My coworker, Sam, is American, but his parents are Nigerian. My English class is on Tuesday and Thursday nights. My classmate Ana is Mexican. She is very funny! English is difficult, but I'm not afraid. Are you OK? Is Carlos busy at work?
>
> Love (con cariño),
> Lucía

## D · Writing / Escritura

**EN:** Write a message to someone in your family or a friend about your home and your English class. Write **7–8 sentences**. Use Lucía's message as your model. Use one skeleton for each sentence.

**ES:** Escribe un mensaje a alguien de tu familia o a un amigo o amiga sobre tu casa y tu clase de inglés. Escribe **7–8 oraciones**. Usa el mensaje de Lucía como modelo. Usa un esqueleto para cada oración.

`Hi, NAME!`

1. `How + are + you?` / `I + am + fine / tired / happy.`
2. `My home + is + in + CITY. It + is + big / small / old / new.`
3. `There + is / are + ... + in my home.`
4. `There + isn't / aren't + ...`
5. `My English class + is + on + DAY(S).`
6. `My teacher / classmate + is + NAME. He/She + is + NATIONALITY.`
7. `English + is + easy / difficult, + but + I + am + FEELING.`
8. `Is / Are + ... ?` (a question for your reader)

`Love, YOUR NAME`

*Ideas en español, oraciones en inglés; si no puedes etiquetar S / V / O en tu oración, reconstrúyela.*

### Checklist / Lista de control

- ☐ Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop (.) or a question mark (?).
- ☐ Names, cities, countries, nationalities, languages and days have a capital letter.
- ☐ No empty subject seat: never ✗ *Is cold* or ✗ *Have a park*.
- ☐ The form of *be* matches the subject: *I am, she is, we are*.
- ☐ Feelings and age use *be*, not *have*.
- ☐ I use *there is / there are* 2 times or more.
- ☐ My singular count nouns have *a*, *an*, *the* or *my*; my noncount nouns have no -s.
- ☐ My question flips the seats.
