## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** In Unit 2 you worked on the verb seat with *be*. This unit is about the **noun seats**. A noun changes when there is more than one (*chair → chairs*). A small word often goes before it (*a, an, the* — or nothing). And English has a special machine to say that something **exists** in a place: *there is / there are*, the English *hay*.

**ES:** En la Unidad 2 trabajaste el asiento del verbo con *be*. Esta unidad trata de los **asientos de sustantivo**. Un sustantivo cambia cuando hay más de uno (*chair → chairs*). A menudo va una palabra pequeña antes (*a, an, the* — o nada). Y el inglés tiene una máquina especial para decir que algo **existe** en un lugar: *there is / there are*, el *hay* del inglés.

### A1 · Plurals / Plurales

**EN:** Most nouns add **-s** for the plural. A few groups change their spelling, and a few nouns are **irregular**: you must memorize them.

**ES:** La mayoría de los sustantivos añaden **-s** para el plural. Algunos grupos cambian la ortografía, y algunos sustantivos son **irregulares**: tienes que memorizarlos.

| Rule / Regla | Examples / Ejemplos |
|---|---|
| most nouns → + **s** | room → rooms, chair → chairs |
| -ch, -sh, -s, -x → + **es** | watch → watches, dish → dishes, bus → buses, box → boxes |
| some nouns in -o → + **es** | tomato → tomatoes, potato → potatoes |
| consonant + y → **-ies** | city → cities, baby → babies |
| vowel + y → + **s** | key → keys, day → days, boy → boys |
| -f / -fe → **-ves** | shelf → shelves, knife → knives, wife → wives |

**EN:** The **-es** ending adds a small sound, like *iz*: *bus-es, box-es, dish-es*. Other plurals add only the *s*, not a new syllable: *room-s, chair-s*.

**ES:** La terminación **-es** añade un sonido pequeño, como *iz*: *bus-es, box-es, dish-es*. Los otros plurales solo añaden la *s*, no una sílaba nueva: *room-s, chair-s*.

**Irregular plurals — memorize / Plurales irregulares — memorízalos**

| Singular | Plural | Español |
|---|---|---|
| man | **men** | hombre → hombres |
| woman | **women** | mujer → mujeres |
| child | **children** | niño/a → niños |
| person | **people** | persona → personas |
| foot | **feet** | pie → pies |
| tooth | **teeth** | diente → dientes |

**EN:** ⚠ *People* and *children* are **already plural**. Never add -s, and use **are**: *The people **are** nice. The children **are** at school.* ✗ *peoples*, ✗ *childrens*, ✗ *the people is*.

**ES:** ⚠ *People* y *children* **ya son plurales**. Nunca añadas -s, y usa **are**: *The people **are** nice* (La gente es amable). *The children **are** at school.* ✗ *peoples*, ✗ *childrens*, ✗ *the people is*. Cuidado: en español *la gente* es singular, pero *people* es plural.

### A2 · Count and noncount nouns / Sustantivos contables e incontables

**EN:** English divides nouns into two families, and the family decides many things: plural or not, *a/an* or not, *is* or *are*.

* **Count nouns** — things you can count one by one: *one chair, two chairs*. They have a plural, and they can take *a/an*.
* **Noncount nouns** — things you can't count one by one: liquids and materials (*water, milk, rice, bread*) and "big groups" or ideas (*money, music, furniture, information, advice, homework, news*). They have **no plural**, **never** take *a/an*, and take **is**.

**ES:** El inglés divide los sustantivos en dos familias, y la familia decide muchas cosas: plural o no, *a/an* o no, *is* o *are*.

* **Contables** — cosas que puedes contar una por una: *one chair, two chairs*. Tienen plural y pueden llevar *a/an*.
* **Incontables** — cosas que no puedes contar una por una: líquidos y materiales (*water, milk, rice, bread*) y "grupos grandes" o ideas (*money, music, furniture, information, advice, homework, news*). **No tienen plural**, **nunca** llevan *a/an* y llevan **is**.

| SUBJECT | VERB | DESCRIPTION | Family |
|---|---|---|---|
| The chair | is | new. | count, one |
| The chairs | are | new. | count, plural |
| The furniture | is | new. | noncount → *is* |
| The milk | is | cold. | noncount → *is* |

**EN:** To count a noncount noun, count a **unit** or a **container**: *a piece of furniture* (un mueble), *a glass of water* (un vaso de agua), *a cup of coffee* (una taza de café). You learn more containers in Unit 14.

**ES:** Para contar un sustantivo incontable, cuenta una **unidad** o un **recipiente**: *a piece of furniture* (un mueble), *a glass of water* (un vaso de agua), *a cup of coffee* (una taza de café). Aprendes más recipientes en la Unidad 14.

**⚠ Countable in Spanish, noncount in English / Contable en español, incontable en inglés**

**EN:** These nouns have a plural in Spanish but not in English. They are very common mistakes. Learn them by heart.

**ES:** Estos sustantivos tienen plural en español, pero no en inglés. Son errores muy comunes. Apréndelos de memoria.

| Español (plural ✓) | English (noncount) | ✓ How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| los muebles | furniture | There is a lot of furniture. / a piece of furniture |
| las tareas | homework | Mateo has a lot of homework. |
| los consejos | advice | Mrs. Park gives good advice. |
| las informaciones | information | I need some information. |
| las noticias | news | The news is good! (news + *is*) |
| los panes | bread | There is some bread. |

### A3 · a / an / the / nothing / a / an / the / nada

**EN:** Articles answer one question: **does the listener know which one I mean?**

**ES:** Los artículos responden una pregunta: **¿el oyente sabe de cuál hablo?**

**1. *a / an* = one, but you don't know which one yet / uno, pero todavía no sabes cuál**

**EN:** Use *a / an* with **one** count noun: the first time you talk about it, or any one of a group. Also with jobs (Unit 2): *She's a nurse.* Choose by the **sound**, not the letter: **an** before a vowel sound, **a** before a consonant sound.

**ES:** Usa *a / an* con **un** sustantivo contable: la primera vez que hablas de él, o cualquiera de un grupo. También con los trabajos (Unidad 2): *She's a nurse.* Elige por el **sonido**, no por la letra: **an** antes de un sonido de vocal, **a** antes de un sonido de consonante.

| a + consonant sound | an + vowel sound |
|---|---|
| a bed, a kitchen | an apartment, an elevator |
| a new lamp | an old lamp |
| a house | an hour (the *h* is silent / la *h* no suena) |
| a university (sounds like "yu") | an umbrella |

**2. *the* = the one we both know / el que los dos conocemos**

**EN:** Use *the* for a specific thing: the **second** time you talk about it, or when there is only one. *There is a sofa in the living room. **The** sofa is old.* *The sun. The park near our building.* *The* works with singular, plural and noncount nouns.

**ES:** Usa *the* para una cosa específica: la **segunda** vez que hablas de ella, o cuando hay solo una. *There is a sofa in the living room. **The** sofa is old* (Hay un sofá en la sala. El sofá es viejo). *The sun. The park near our building.* *The* funciona con singular, plural e incontables.

**3. Nothing = things in general / Nada = cosas en general**

**EN:** When you talk about things **in general**, use a plural or a noncount noun with **no article**: *Mateo loves **dinosaurs**. **Coffee** is expensive. I like **music**.*

**ES:** Cuando hablas de cosas **en general**, usa un plural o un incontable **sin artículo**: *Mateo loves **dinosaurs*** (A Mateo le encantan los dinosaurios). ***Coffee** is expensive* (El café es caro). *I like **music*** (Me gusta la música).

**⚠ The general-statement trap / La trampa de las generalizaciones**

**EN:** Spanish uses *el / la / los / las* for general statements. English uses **nothing**. Use *the* only for a specific thing.

**ES:** El español usa *el / la / los / las* para las generalizaciones. El inglés **no usa nada**. Usa *the* solo para una cosa específica.

| Español | ✗ (general) | ✓ |
|---|---|---|
| Me gusta el café. | ~~I like the coffee.~~ | I like **coffee**. |
| Los muebles son caros. | ~~The furniture is expensive.~~ | **Furniture** is expensive. |
| Los niños son curiosos. | ~~The children are curious.~~ | **Children** are curious. |

**EN:** (*I like the coffee* is correct only for a specific coffee: *I like the coffee at the Blue Door Café.*)

**ES:** (*I like the coffee* es correcto solo para un café específico: *I like the coffee at the Blue Door Café* — Me gusta el café del Blue Door Café.)

**Summary / Resumen**

| | One (count) | Plural (count) | Noncount |
|---|---|---|---|
| new, not specific | a chair / an old chair | chairs | furniture |
| specific | the chair | the chairs | the furniture |
| in general | — | Chairs are… | Furniture is… |
| ✗ never | ~~chair~~ alone | ~~a chairs~~ | ~~a furniture~~, ~~furnitures~~ |

### A4 · *There is / There are* = *hay*

**EN:** To say that something **exists** in a place, English uses *there is* (one thing, or noncount) and *there are* (plural). Spanish uses one word for both — *hay* — so choosing *is* or *are* is the new skill. *There* sits in the subject seat, but the real "boss" is the noun **after** the verb: ★ **the noun after the verb decides *is* or *are*.**

**ES:** Para decir que algo **existe** en un lugar, el inglés usa *there is* (una cosa, o un incontable) y *there are* (plural). El español usa una palabra para los dos — *hay* — así que elegir *is* o *are* es la habilidad nueva. *There* se sienta en el asiento del sujeto, pero el verdadero "jefe" es el sustantivo **después** del verbo: ★ **el sustantivo después del verbo decide *is* o *are*.**

| SUBJECT | VERB | NOUN (the boss) | PLACE |
|---|---|---|---|
| There | is | a park | near the building. |
| There | are | two bedrooms | in the apartment. |
| There | is | some milk | in the fridge. |
| A park | is | — | near the building. |

**EN:** (The last row has an ordinary noun in the subject seat. It is correct too, but for new information English prefers *there is*.)

**ES:** (La última fila tiene un sustantivo normal en el asiento del sujeto. También es correcta, pero para información nueva el inglés prefiere *there is*.)

**Negatives and questions / Negaciones y preguntas**

**EN:** *There is / there are* works like *be* in Unit 2: add **not** for the negative, and **flip** for the question. In negatives and questions with a plural or a noncount noun, we often add **any** (≈ *ningún / algún*). You study *any* in Unit 14.

**ES:** *There is / there are* funciona como *be* en la Unidad 2: añade **not** para la negación e **invierte** para la pregunta. En negaciones y preguntas con un plural o un incontable, a menudo añadimos **any** (≈ *ningún / algún*). Estudias *any* en la Unidad 14.

| | One / noncount | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| + | There **is** (There's) a sofa. | There **are** two beds. |
| − | There **isn't** an elevator. | There **aren't** any chairs. |
| ? | **Is there** a TV? | **Are there** any shops? |
| Yes | Yes, there is. | Yes, there are. |
| No | No, there isn't. | No, there aren't. |

**EN:** As in Unit 2, never end a *yes* answer with a short form: ✗ *Yes, there's.* → ✓ *Yes, there is.*

**ES:** Como en la Unidad 2, nunca termines una respuesta con *yes* con una contracción: ✗ *Yes, there's.* → ✓ *Yes, there is.*

**⚠ Three Spanish traps with *hay* / Tres trampas del español con *hay***

**EN:**

1. *Hay* is not *have*: ✗ *Have a park near my house.* → ✓ ***There is** a park near my house.*
2. Don't leave the subject seat empty (Unit 1): ✗ *Is a table in the kitchen.* → ✓ ***There** is a table in the kitchen.*
3. First *there is*, then *it* or *the*: *Hay un parque. Es grande.* → ✓ *There is **a** park. **It** is big.* / *The park is big.*

**ES:** 1. *Hay* no es *have*: ✗ *Have a park near my house.* → ✓ ***There is** a park near my house.* 2. No dejes vacío el asiento del sujeto (Unidad 1): ✗ *Is a table in the kitchen.* → ✓ ***There** is a table in the kitchen.* 3. Primero *there is*, luego *it* o *the*: *Hay un parque. Es grande.* → ✓ *There is **a** park. **It** is big.* / *The park is big.*

### A5 · *some* and *a lot of* / *some* y *a lot of*

**EN:** Two words tell you "how much" without a number. Both work with **plurals** and **noncount** nouns.

* **some** = a small number or amount, not exact (≈ *unos, algo de*): *There are some chairs. There is some milk.*
* **a lot of** = a big number or amount (≈ *muchos, mucho*): *There are a lot of books. There is a lot of furniture.*

**ES:** Dos palabras dicen "cuánto" sin un número. Las dos funcionan con **plurales** e **incontables**.

* **some** = un número o una cantidad pequeña, no exacta (≈ *unos, algo de*): *There are some chairs. There is some milk.*
* **a lot of** = un número o una cantidad grande (≈ *muchos, mucho*): *There are a lot of books. There is a lot of furniture.*

| | Plural (count) | Noncount |
|---|---|---|
| some | some chairs | some milk |
| a lot of | a lot of books | a lot of furniture |
| ✗ never | ~~some chair~~ | ~~a lot of furnitures~~ |

**EN:** Remember: the noun decides the verb. *A lot of books* → *there **are***. *A lot of furniture* → *there **is***. You meet *How much? / How many?* in Unit 9, and more quantity words (*any, much, many*) in Unit 14.

**ES:** Recuerda: el sustantivo decide el verbo. *A lot of books* → *there **are***. *A lot of furniture* → *there **is***. Conoces *How much? / How many?* en la Unidad 9, y más palabras de cantidad (*any, much, many*) en la Unidad 14.

### A6 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I have a lot of furnitures. | I have a lot of **furniture**. | noncount — no plural |
| Two persons live here. | Two **people** live here. | irregular plural |
| The childrens are here. | The **children** are here. | *children* is already plural |
| The people is nice. | The people **are** nice. | *people* is plural → *are* |
| Have a park near my house. | **There is** a park near my house. | *hay* = *there is/are* |
| Is a table in the kitchen. | **There** is a table in the kitchen. | subject seat never empty |
| There are a sofa. | There **is** a sofa. | the noun after decides |
| I like the music. (general) | I like **music**. | general → no article |
| a apartment / an house | **an** apartment / **a** house | the sound decides |
| I need an information. | I need **some information**. | noncount — no *a/an* |

### Word tip / Consejo de palabras

**EN:** *Carpet* does **not** mean *carpeta* — it means *alfombra*. For *carpeta*, say *folder*. Also careful with *floor*: it means the level of a building (*piso*) **and** the ground you walk on (*suelo*). And in the United States, the **first floor** is the street level — the *planta baja* in many Latin American countries. So Lucía's apartment on the third floor is two levels above the street.

**ES:** *Carpet* **no** significa *carpeta* — significa *alfombra*. Para *carpeta*, di *folder*. Cuidado también con *floor*: significa el nivel de un edificio (*piso*) **y** lo que pisas (*suelo*). Y en Estados Unidos, el **first floor** es el nivel de la calle — la *planta baja* en muchos países de América Latina. Así que el apartamento de Lucía en el *third floor* está dos niveles arriba de la calle.
