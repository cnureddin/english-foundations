## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** In Unit 13 you learned the modals and their law: **modal + base verb**. This unit gives the modals a **social job**: being polite when you ask, offer and invite. Then we go back to the count and noncount nouns of Unit 3 and learn to talk about **quantities**: *some, any, much, many, a bottle of…* You need all of this at the supermarket, in a restaurant, and at a friend's table.

**ES:** En la Unidad 13 aprendiste los modales y su ley: **modal + verbo base**. Esta unidad les da a los modales un **trabajo social**: ser cortés cuando pides, ofreces e invitas. Después volvemos a los sustantivos contables e incontables de la Unidad 3 y aprendemos a hablar de **cantidades**: *some, any, much, many, a bottle of…* Necesitas todo esto en el supermercado, en un restaurante y en la mesa de un amigo.

### A1 · Polite machines: one law, five jobs / Máquinas de cortesía: una ley, cinco trabajos

**EN:** English has a fixed frame for each social job. Learn them as **whole frames**, not word by word: they are the sentences you will say most often in real life. They all obey the law of Unit 13: modal + base verb, and the modal flips to make a question.

**ES:** El inglés tiene un marco fijo para cada trabajo social. Apréndelos como **marcos completos**, no palabra por palabra: son las oraciones que más vas a decir en la vida real. Todos obedecen la ley de la Unidad 13: modal + verbo base, y el modal se invierte para preguntar.

| Job / Trabajo | Frame / Marco | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. ask someone to do something | **Could you** + verb…? | Could you open the door, please? | ¿Podrías abrir la puerta, por favor? |
| 2. ask for a thing | **Could I have** + noun…? | Could I have the menu, please? | ¿Me da el menú, por favor? |
| 3. ask permission | **Can / Could I** + verb…? | Can I pay by card? | ¿Puedo pagar con tarjeta? |
| 4. offer or invite | **Would you like** + noun? | Would you like some tea? | ¿Quieres un té? |
| | **Would you like to** + verb? | Would you like to come for dinner? | ¿Te gustaría venir a cenar? |
| 5. say what you want | **I'd like** + noun / to + verb | I'd like a coffee, please. | Quisiera un café, por favor. |

**EN:** Look at the seats of a polite question. The helper jumps to the front; the subject and the main verb stay in order:

**ES:** Mira los asientos de una pregunta cortés. El auxiliar salta adelante; el sujeto y el verbo principal se quedan en su orden:

| HELPER | SUBJECT | MAIN VERB | OBJECT / REST |
|---|---|---|---|
| Could | you | open | the door, please? |
| Could | I | have | the menu, please? |
| Would | you | like | some tea? |
| — | You | open | the door. *(statement / afirmación)* |

**★ Could is the volume knob / Could es la perilla del volumen:** *can* = normal, *could* = more polite. Same meaning, softer sound. With strangers, in shops and at work: **could** + **please**. / *can* = normal, *could* = más cortés. Mismo significado, sonido más suave. Con desconocidos, en tiendas y en el trabajo: **could** + **please**.

**EN:** How to answer. To an offer: ***Yes, please.*** / ***No, thanks.*** To a request: ***Sure.*** / ***Of course.*** When you give something to someone: ***Here you are.***

**ES:** Cómo responder. A una oferta: ***Yes, please.*** / ***No, thanks.*** A una petición: ***Sure.*** / ***Of course.*** Cuando le das algo a alguien: ***Here you are.*** (Aquí tiene.)

**Shall I…? and Let's… / Shall I…? y Let's…**

**EN:** Two more short frames. **Shall I + base verb?** offers to do something **for** another person. **Let's + base verb** suggests an activity for **us** (you and me). No *to* after *Let's*.

**ES:** Dos marcos cortos más. **Shall I + verbo base?** ofrece hacer algo **por** otra persona. **Let's + verbo base** sugiere una actividad para **nosotros** (tú y yo). Sin *to* después de *Let's*.

| Frame / Marco | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|
| Shall I + verb? | Shall I bring some bread? | ¿Llevo pan? / ¿Quieres que lleve pan? |
| Shall I + verb? | Shall I close the window? | ¿Cierro la ventana? |
| Let's + verb | Let's go to the market. | Vamos al mercado. |
| Let's + verb | Let's make arepas! | ¡Hagamos arepas! |

### A2 · The two traps / Las dos trampas

**⚠ Trap 1 — *I want* sounds too direct / *I want* suena demasiado directo**

**EN:** In Spanish, *quiero un café* is normal in a café. In English, ***I want** a coffee* sounds like a child or an order. Use ***I'd like*** (= *I would like*): *I'd like a coffee, please.*

**ES:** En español, *quiero un café* es normal en un café. En inglés, ***I want** a coffee* suena como un niño o como una orden. Usa ***I'd like*** (= *I would like*): *I'd like a coffee, please.* (Quisiera un café, por favor.)

**⚠ Trap 2 — *Can you give me…?* is less polite / *Can you give me…?* es menos cortés**

**EN:** In Spanish, we often say *¿Me das…?* or *¿Me puedes dar…?* In English, *Can you give me the salt?* is correct grammar, but it sounds a little rude, and *Give me the salt* is an order. To ask for a **thing**, put **I** in the subject seat: ***Could I have** the salt, please?*

**ES:** En español decimos a menudo *¿Me das…?* o *¿Me puedes dar…?* En inglés, *Can you give me the salt?* es gramática correcta, pero suena un poco brusco, y *Give me the salt* es una orden. Para pedir una **cosa**, pon **I** en el asiento del sujeto: ***Could I have** the salt, please?*

**⚠ And one more: *Would you like* ≠ *Do you like* / Y una más**

**EN:** *Do you like cake?* asks about your taste **in general** (Unit 5). *Would you like some cake?* is an **offer, now**. Spanish makes the same difference: *¿Te gusta…?* = *Do you like…?* · *¿Te gustaría…? / ¿Quieres…?* = *Would you like…?* So if someone offers cake, don't answer *Yes, I like cake*. Say ***Yes, please.***

**ES:** *Do you like cake?* pregunta por tu gusto **en general** (Unidad 5). *Would you like some cake?* es una **oferta, ahora**. El español hace la misma diferencia: *¿Te gusta…?* = *Do you like…?* · *¿Te gustaría…? / ¿Quieres…?* = *Would you like…?* Así que si alguien te ofrece pastel, no respondas *Yes, I like cake*. Di ***Yes, please.***

| ✗ Too direct / Muy directo | ✓ Polite / Cortés |
|---|---|
| I want the menu. | I'd like the menu, please. / Could I have the menu, please? |
| Give me a glass of water. | Could I have a glass of water, please? |
| Can you give me the check? | Could I have the check, please? |
| Open the window. | Could you open the window, please? |

### A3 · Some and any / Some y any

**EN:** In Unit 3 you learned **count** nouns (*an egg, two eggs*) and **noncount** nouns (*milk, rice, bread, money*). **Some** and **any** say "an amount" — not an exact number. They work with **plural** count nouns and with **noncount** nouns. The type of sentence decides which one you use:

**ES:** En la Unidad 3 aprendiste los sustantivos **contables** (*an egg, two eggs*) e **incontables** (*milk, rice, bread, money*). **Some** y **any** dicen "una cantidad" — no un número exacto. Funcionan con sustantivos contables en **plural** y con sustantivos **incontables**. El tipo de oración decide cuál usas:

| Sentence / Oración | Word | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| + | **some** | We need some eggs. There is some milk. | Necesitamos huevos. Hay leche. |
| − | **any** | We don't need any eggs. There isn't any milk. | No necesitamos huevos. No hay leche. |
| ? | **any** | Do we have any eggs? Is there any milk? | ¿Tenemos huevos? ¿Hay leche? |
| offer / request ? | **some** | Would you like some tea? Could I have some water? | ¿Quieres té? ¿Me da agua? |

**★ Rule / Regla:** *some* in positive sentences, *any* in negatives and questions — but ***some*** in offers and requests (you expect a *yes*).

**EN:** *Some* and *any* sit **before the noun**, inside the object seat. The noun is still the boss of the seat:

**ES:** *Some* y *any* se sientan **antes del sustantivo**, dentro del asiento del objeto. El sustantivo sigue siendo el jefe del asiento:

| SUBJECT / SUJETO | VERB / VERBO | OBJECT / OBJETO | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| We | need | **some** eggs. | Necesitamos (unos) huevos. |
| We | don't need | **any** eggs. | No necesitamos huevos. |
| We | need | eggs. | *(plain noun / sustantivo solo)* |

**⚠ Spanish trap / Trampa del español:** Spanish often uses **no word** here: *¿Tienes huevos? No hay leche.* English usually adds *any*: *Do you have **any** eggs? There isn't **any** milk.* And with **one** count thing, use *a / an*, not *some*: ✗ *some egg* → ✓ *an egg*. / El español muchas veces no usa **ninguna palabra** aquí. El inglés normalmente añade *any*. Y con **una** cosa contable, usa *a / an*, no *some*.

### A4 · Much, many, a lot of · How much? How many? / Much, many, a lot of · ¿Cuánto? ¿Cuántos?

**EN:** You met *How much* and *How many* in Unit 9. Here is the full picture. The noun decides: **many** for count nouns, **much** for noncount nouns. **A lot of** works with both.

**ES:** Conociste *How much* y *How many* en la Unidad 9. Aquí está el cuadro completo. El sustantivo decide: **many** para contables, **much** para incontables. **A lot of** funciona con los dos.

| Sentence / Oración | Count / Contable (eggs) | Noncount / Incontable (milk) |
|---|---|---|
| + | a lot of eggs | a lot of milk |
| − | not many eggs | not much milk |
| ? | How many eggs? | How much milk? |

**EN:** In positive sentences, *a lot of* sounds natural. *Much* lives mostly in negatives and questions: ✗ *I have much money.* (strange) → ✓ *I have a lot of money.* / *I don't have much money.*

**ES:** En oraciones positivas, *a lot of* suena natural. *Much* vive sobre todo en negaciones y preguntas: ✗ *I have much money.* (raro) → ✓ *I have a lot of money.* / *I don't have much money.*

**Prices / Precios**

**EN:** To ask a price, use ***How much***: *How much is it? How much are they?* You don't need the word *cost*, but *How much does it cost?* is also correct.

**ES:** Para preguntar un precio, usa ***How much***: *How much is it?* (¿Cuánto cuesta?) *How much are they?* (¿Cuánto cuestan?) No necesitas la palabra *cost*, pero *How much does it cost?* también es correcto.

| Question / Pregunta | Answer / Respuesta | Say it / Dilo así |
|---|---|---|
| How much is the cheese? | It's $4.50. | "four fifty" / "four dollars and fifty cents" |
| How much are the apples? | They're $2 a pound. | "two dollars a pound" |
| How much is this juice? | It's $12.99. | "twelve ninety-nine" |
| How much is a banana? | It's 75¢. | "seventy-five cents" |

**⚠ Spanish trap / Trampa del español:** Spanish writes *4,50 $* or *$4,50*; English writes **$4.50** — a point, not a comma, and the $ goes first. / El español escribe *4,50 $* o *$4,50*; el inglés escribe **$4.50** — punto, no coma, y el $ va primero.

**Mini-practice 3 / Mini-práctica 3** — Write *much* or *many*. / Escribe *much* o *many*.

1. How ___ eggs do we need?
2. How ___ is this cheese?
3. We don't have ___ time.
4. How ___ bottles of water did you buy?
5. There isn't ___ sugar in my coffee.

### A5 · Counting noncount things / Contar cosas incontables

**EN:** You can't say ✗ *two breads* or ✗ *a milk*. To count a noncount noun, count its **container** or a **unit**. The container takes the plural, not the food: *two bottles of water*.

**ES:** No puedes decir ✗ *two breads* ni ✗ *a milk*. Para contar un sustantivo incontable, cuenta su **recipiente** o una **unidad**. El recipiente lleva el plural, no la comida: *two bottles of water*.

| Container / unit | + noun | Español |
|---|---|---|
| a bottle of | water, juice, oil | una botella de |
| a cup of | coffee, tea | una taza de |
| a glass of | milk, water | un vaso de |
| a piece of | cake, cheese, advice | un pedazo de, un trozo de |
| a slice of | bread, pizza | una rebanada de |
| a loaf of (loaves of) | bread | un pan entero |
| a bag of | rice, flour | una bolsa de |
| a carton of | milk, eggs | un cartón de |
| a can of | soda, beans | una lata de |
| a pound of | meat, apples | una libra de *(the US uses pounds / EE. UU. usa libras)* |

**EN:** One exception you will hear: in a café, people order *a coffee* or *two coffees* — it means *a cup of coffee*. That's fine for ordering drinks.

**ES:** Una excepción que vas a oír: en un café, la gente pide *a coffee* o *two coffees* — significa *una taza de café*. Eso está bien para pedir bebidas.

### A6 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I want a coffee. | **I'd like** a coffee, please. | *I want* is too direct |
| Give me the check. | **Could I have** the check, please? | a command is rude |
| Would you like to some tea? | Would you like **some tea**? | *would you like* + noun: no *to* |
| Let's to go. | Let's **go**. | *Let's* + base verb |
| There isn't some milk. | There isn't **any** milk. | negative → *any* |
| How many money? | How **much** money? | *money* is noncount |
| I bought three breads. | I bought three **loaves of bread**. | count the container |
| two bottle of water | two **bottles** of water | the container is plural |
| Yes, I like. (to an offer) | **Yes, please.** | an offer is not about taste |

### Word tip / Consejo de palabras

**EN:** In a US restaurant, *la cuenta* is usually **the check**; *the bill* is also correct. But in the US, a **bill** is also paper money (*a ten-dollar bill* = un billete de diez dólares) and a paper you must pay (*the phone bill*). And in the US, a **tip** of 15–20% is normal in restaurants: it is not included in the price.

**ES:** En un restaurante de EE. UU., *la cuenta* es normalmente **the check**; *the bill* también es correcto. Pero en EE. UU., **bill** también es un billete (*a ten-dollar bill* = un billete de diez dólares) y una factura que tienes que pagar (*the phone bill* = la cuenta del teléfono). Y en EE. UU., una **propina** (*tip*) del 15–20 % es normal en los restaurantes: no está incluida en el precio.
