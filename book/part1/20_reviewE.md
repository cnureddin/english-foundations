### Learning a Language as an Adult

*Ask Mr. Miller · Lincoln Adult School News*

> Every month, a student asks me the same question: "I'm an adult. Can I really learn English?" My answer is always the same: yes, you can! Children can copy new sounds easily, but adults have other strengths (*fortalezas*). You can read, you can plan, and you know how to study. Here is my advice.
>
> First, you should practice a little every day. Studying for fifteen minutes every morning is a great habit. You don't have to study for three hours on Sunday!
>
> Second, use your hobbies. Do you enjoy cooking? Find recipes in English. Do you love watching soccer? Listen to the game in English. Are you interested in music? Read the words of your favorite songs and sing them.
>
> Third, go shopping in English. At the supermarket, read the labels: *a carton of milk, a bag of rice*. At a café, don't say "I want a coffee." Say "Could I have a coffee, please?" Every conversation is a small class.
>
> Fourth, you mustn't be afraid of mistakes. Everybody makes them. You don't have to speak perfectly — but you have to speak!
>
> One of my students writes a blog in English. In her first posts, she could only write five or six sentences. Now her posts are long and funny, and she has a lot of readers.
>
> Finally, you should be kind to yourself. Learning a language takes time, and you can't learn everything in one week. Don't stop. Keep going!
>
> *Do you have a question for Mr. Miller? Leave it at the school office.*

## D · Writing / Escritura

### Model / Modelo

**EN:** Lucía answers Mr. Miller's column with her own advice.

**ES:** Lucía responde a la columna de Mr. Miller con sus propios consejos.

> To learn English, you should practice every day. I study for twenty minutes on the bus to work. Listening to songs in English helps me a lot. I love cooking, and I often read recipes in English. You shouldn't translate every word. You don't have to understand everything! At the hotel, I have to speak English with guests all day, and it is a great class. Next month, I'm going to watch a series with English subtitles. My advice: don't be afraid of mistakes!

### Your turn / Tu turno

**EN:** What should you do to learn English faster (*más rápido*)? Write **9–10 sentences** with your opinion and your own experience. Use modals (Unit 13), at least one polite frame or *some/any* (Unit 14), and gerunds and infinitives (Unit 15). Use one skeleton for each sentence.
**ES:** ¿Qué deberías hacer para aprender inglés más rápido? Escribe **9–10 oraciones** con tu opinión y tu propia experiencia. Usa modales (Unidad 13), al menos un marco cortés o *some/any* (Unidad 14), y gerunds e infinitivos (Unidad 15). Usa un esqueleto para cada oración.

1. `To learn English, you + should + VERB + ...`
2. `I + study / practice + ... + on DAY / at TIME.`
3. `VERB-ing + ... + helps me + VERB.`
4. `I + enjoy / love + VERB-ing + ... in English.`
5. `You + shouldn't + VERB + ...`
6. `You + don't have to + VERB + ...`
7. `At WORK / At the store, I + have to / can + VERB + ...`
8. `In a café, say: "Could I have + ..., please?"`
9. `Next month, I + am going to / want to + VERB + ...`
10. `My advice: don't + VERB + ...!`

*Ideas en español, oraciones en inglés; si no puedes etiquetar S / V / O en tu oración, reconstrúyela.*

### Checklist / Lista de control

- ☐ Every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with (.), (?) or (!).
- ☐ After every modal: a base verb (no *to*, no *-s*).
- ☐ *mustn't* = prohibited; *don't have to* = not necessary.
- ☐ Activities in noun seats wear a costume: *-ing* or *to* + verb.
- ☐ After a preposition: *-ing*. Purpose: *to*, never *for* + verb.
- ☐ I used at least 3 modals, 2 gerunds and 2 infinitives.
- ☐ I can label S, V, O in every sentence.
