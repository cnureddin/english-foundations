## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** Until now, all your English lived in the present: what you do every day (Unit 5) and what is happening now (Unit 8). This unit opens the door to the **past**. With the past you can tell a story: your weekend, your day, your life. This week you learn the **regular** verbs (they take **-ed**) and the past of *be* (**was / were**). In Unit 11 you learn the irregular verbs and the full machine for negatives and questions.

**ES:** Hasta ahora, todo tu inglés vivía en el presente: lo que haces cada día (Unidad 5) y lo que está pasando ahora (Unidad 8). Esta unidad abre la puerta al **pasado**. Con el pasado puedes contar una historia: tu fin de semana, tu día, tu vida. Esta semana aprendes los verbos **regulares** (llevan **-ed**) y el pasado de *be* (**was / were**). En la Unidad 11 aprendes los verbos irregulares y la máquina completa para negaciones y preguntas.

### A1 · What the past does / Para qué sirve el pasado

**EN:** The simple past talks about actions and states that are **finished**, at a **past time**: *I cleaned the kitchen yesterday.* The action started and ended. It is finished. Often a time word tells you when: *yesterday, last night, two days ago, in 2020*.

**ES:** El pasado simple habla de acciones y estados **terminados**, en un **momento pasado**: *I cleaned the kitchen yesterday* (Limpié la cocina ayer). La acción empezó y terminó. Está terminada. Muchas veces una palabra de tiempo te dice cuándo: *yesterday, last night, two days ago, in 2020*.

| Present (Unit 5) | Past (Unit 10) | Español |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| I work at the Lakeview Hotel. | I worked at the Blue Door Café. | Trabajo… / Trabajé (trabajaba)… |
| Mateo plays soccer every day. | Mateo played soccer yesterday. | Juega… / Jugó… |
| We watch TV at night. | We watched a movie last night. | Vemos… / Vimos… |
| It is cold today. | It was cold yesterday. | Hace frío… / Hizo frío… |

**⚠ Good news for Spanish speakers / Buena noticia para hispanohablantes**

**EN:** Spanish has two past tenses: *trabajé* (pretérito) and *trabajaba* (imperfecto). In most everyday sentences, English uses **one** form for both: *worked*. *I worked in a café* = *Trabajé en un café* or *Trabajaba en un café*. You don't need to choose.

**ES:** El español tiene dos pasados: *trabajé* (pretérito) y *trabajaba* (imperfecto). En la mayoría de las oraciones cotidianas, el inglés usa **una** sola forma para los dos: *worked*. *I worked in a café* = *Trabajé en un café* o *Trabajaba en un café*. No necesitas elegir.

### A2 · Regular verbs: + -ed / Verbos regulares: + -ed

**EN:** Most English verbs make the past with **-ed**: *work → worked, clean → cleaned*. The seats don't change. Only the word in the verb seat changes.

**ES:** La mayoría de los verbos en inglés forman el pasado con **-ed**: *work → worked, clean → cleaned*. Los asientos no cambian. Solo cambia la palabra del asiento del verbo.

| SUBJECT / SUJETO | VERB / VERBO (past) | OBJECT / OBJETO | Español |
|-------------------|-------------------|---------------|--------------------------------------|
| I | cleaned | the kitchen. | Limpié la cocina. |
| Mateo | played | soccer. | Mateo jugó al fútbol. |
| Lucía and Mrs. Park | watched | a movie. | Lucía y Mrs. Park vieron una película. |
| They | visited | the library. | Visitaron la biblioteca. |

**★ One form for every subject / Una forma para todos los sujetos**

**EN:** This is a beautiful simplification: in the past, the verb is the **same for every subject**. No -s for *he / she / it*. *I worked, you worked, she worked, we worked, they worked.*

**ES:** Es una simplificación hermosa: en el pasado, el verbo es **igual para todos los sujetos**. Sin -s para *he / she / it*. *I worked, you worked, she worked, we worked, they worked.*

**Spelling / Ortografía**

| Rule / Regla | Base → past | More examples / Más ejemplos |
|---------------------------------------------|---------------|--------------------------------|
| most verbs: + **-ed** | work → worked | watch → watched, clean → cleaned |
| ends in -e: + **-d** | live → lived | dance → danced, move → moved |
| consonant + y: y → **-ied** | study → studied | try → tried, carry → carried |
| vowel + y: + **-ed** | play → played | stay → stayed, enjoy → enjoyed |
| short verb, consonant-vowel-consonant: **double** | stop → stopped | plan → planned, shop → shopped |

**EN:** Two notes. (1) Never double *w*, *x* or *y*: *snow → snowed, relax → relaxed, play → played*. (2) Longer verbs with the stress on the first part don't double: *visit → visited, listen → listened*. In American English, *travel → traveled* (one *l*).

**ES:** Dos notas. (1) Nunca dupliques *w*, *x* ni *y*: *snow → snowed, relax → relaxed, play → played*. (2) Los verbos más largos con el acento en la primera parte no duplican: *visit → visited, listen → listened*. En inglés americano, *travel → traveled* (una sola *l*).

### A3 · The three sounds of -ed / Los tres sonidos de -ed

**EN:** You write **-ed** in every regular verb, but you don't always say it the same way. There are three sounds. Only one of them adds an extra syllable.

**ES:** Escribes **-ed** en todos los verbos regulares, pero no siempre lo pronuncias igual. Hay tres sonidos. Solo uno de ellos agrega una sílaba extra.

| Sound / Sonido | When / Cuándo | Examples | Sounds like |
|--------------|-----------------------------|----------------------------------------|-------------------------|
| **/t/** | after p, k, s, sh, ch, f, x | walked, cooked, watched, danced, stopped | "uokt", "cukt" |
| **/d/** | after vowels and other sounds | played, cleaned, called, lived, studied | "pleid", "klind" |
| **/ɪd/** | after **t** or **d** only | visited, wanted, needed, started, ended | "vi-si-tid" (+1 syllable) |

**⚠ The extra syllable / La sílaba extra**

**EN:** Spanish speakers often read *-ed* as a new syllable every time: ✗ "walk-ed", ✗ "cook-ed". This is the most common pronunciation mistake. The extra syllable comes **only after t or d**: *vi-si-ted, star-ted, nee-ded*. *Walked* has one syllable. *Danced* sounds like "danst".

**ES:** Los hispanohablantes muchas veces leen *-ed* como una sílaba nueva siempre: ✗ "walk-ed", ✗ "cook-ed". Es el error de pronunciación más común. La sílaba extra aparece **solo después de t o d**: *vi-si-ted, star-ted, nee-ded*. *Walked* tiene una sola sílaba. *Danced* suena "danst".

### A4 · *Be* in the past: was / were / *Be* en el pasado: was / were

**EN:** *Be* is special in the past, too. It has two forms: **was** and **were**. It keeps the machine you learned in Unit 2: add *not* for negatives, flip for questions.

**ES:** *Be* también es especial en el pasado. Tiene dos formas: **was** y **were**. Conserva la máquina que aprendiste en la Unidad 2: agrega *not* para negar, invierte para preguntar.

| Subject / Sujeto | Past of *be* | Negative / Negación | Example / Ejemplo |
|-------------------|-----------|---------------------|---------------------------------------|
| I / he / she / it | **was** | wasn't (was not) | I was tired. It wasn't cold. |
| you / we / they | **were** | weren't (were not) | We were at home. They weren't late. |

| SUBJECT / SUJETO | VERB / VERBO | DESCRIPTION / DESCRIPCIÓN | Español |
|-----------------|-------------|--------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| I | was | tired. | Estaba cansado/a. |
| The movie | was | long. | La película fue larga. |
| Mateo and Leo | were | in the park. | Mateo y Leo estaban en el parque. |
| You | weren't | at work. | No estabas en el trabajo. |

**Questions: flip the subject and *be* / Preguntas: invierte el sujeto y *be***

| Question / Pregunta | Short answer / Respuesta corta | Español |
|-----------------------|------------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Was** the movie good? | Yes, it was. / No, it wasn't. | ¿La película estuvo buena? |
| **Were** you at home? | Yes, I was. / No, I wasn't. | ¿Estabas en casa? |
| Where **were** they? | They were at the lake. | ¿Dónde estaban? |

**⚠ The *tener* trap, now in the past / La trampa de *tener*, ahora en pasado**

**EN:** In Unit 2 you learned: *Tengo hambre* = *I am hungry* (not *have*). The past is the same: use **was / were**, not *had*.

**ES:** En la Unidad 2 aprendiste: *Tengo hambre* = *I am hungry* (no *have*). En pasado es igual: usa **was / were**, no *had*.

* *Tenía hambre.* → ✓ *I **was** hungry.*
* *Tenía 20 años.* → ✓ *I **was** 20.*
* *Teníamos frío.* → ✓ *We **were** cold.*

**⚠ *Había* = there was / there were**

**EN:** In Unit 3 you learned *hay* = *there is / there are*. The past is *there was* (one thing) and *there were* (many things). *There was a lot of snow. There were ten guests at the hotel.*

**ES:** En la Unidad 3 aprendiste *hay* = *there is / there are*. El pasado es *there was* (una cosa) y *there were* (muchas cosas). *There was a lot of snow* (Había mucha nieve). *There were ten guests at the hotel* (Había diez huéspedes en el hotel).

### A5 · Past time words / Palabras de tiempo pasado

**EN:** Past time words tell you **when**. They usually go at the end of the sentence, or at the start: *I called my mother yesterday. / Yesterday I called my mother.*

**ES:** Las palabras de tiempo pasado dicen **cuándo**. Normalmente van al final de la oración, o al principio: *I called my mother yesterday. / Yesterday I called my mother.*

| Time word / Palabra | Example / Ejemplo | Español |
|----------------------------------|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| yesterday | I cleaned yesterday. | ayer |
| yesterday morning / afternoon | We walked yesterday afternoon. | ayer por la mañana / la tarde |
| **last night** | I watched TV last night. | anoche |
| last week / weekend / month / year | She visited Ana last week. | la semana pasada, etc. |
| last Saturday / last summer | We traveled last summer. | el sábado pasado / el verano pasado |
| two days **ago** / an hour **ago** | He called an hour ago. | hace dos días / hace una hora |
| the day before yesterday | I cooked the day before yesterday. | anteayer |
| in 2020 / in May | They moved in 2020. | en 2020 / en mayo |
| on Saturday / at 7 p.m. | The class started at 6 p.m. | el sábado / a las 7 (Unit 6) |

**★ No *the*, no preposition before *last* / Sin *the* ni preposición antes de *last***

**EN:** Remember Unit 6: no preposition with *last*. And no *the*. ✗ *in the last week* → ✓ *last week*. ✗ *on last Saturday* → ✓ *last Saturday*.

**ES:** Recuerda la Unidad 6: sin preposición con *last*. Y sin *the*. ✗ *in the last week* → ✓ *last week*. ✗ *on last Saturday* → ✓ *last Saturday*.

**⚠ *Anoche* and *hace* / *Anoche* y *hace***

**EN:** *Anoche* is **last night** — ✗ *yesterday night*. And *hace dos días* has a different order: the time comes first, then **ago** at the end. ✗ *ago two days*, ✗ *makes two days* → ✓ *two days ago*.

**ES:** *Anoche* es **last night** — ✗ *yesterday night*. Y *hace dos días* tiene otro orden: primero el tiempo, y **ago** al final. ✗ *ago two days*, ✗ *makes two days* → ✓ *two days ago*.

### A6 · Preview: the helper *did* / Adelanto: el auxiliar *did*

**EN:** How do you say *No vi la tele* or *¿Limpiaste la cocina?* Normal verbs use a helper in the past: **did** (the past of *do*). It is the same machine as Unit 5 (*do / does*). The helper **carries the past**, so the main verb goes back to its **base form** (no -ed). *Did* is the same for every subject. Here is a first look; Unit 11 teaches the full machine.

**ES:** ¿Cómo se dice *No vi la tele* o *¿Limpiaste la cocina?* Los verbos normales usan un auxiliar en pasado: **did** (el pasado de *do*). Es la misma máquina de la Unidad 5 (*do / does*). El auxiliar **lleva el pasado**, así que el verbo principal vuelve a su **forma base** (sin -ed). *Did* es igual para todos los sujetos. Aquí tienes un primer vistazo; la Unidad 11 enseña la máquina completa.

| Type / Tipo | Example / Ejemplo | Español |
|---|---|---|
| + | Mateo play**ed** soccer. | Mateo jugó al fútbol. |
| − | Mateo **didn't play** soccer. | Mateo no jugó al fútbol. |
| ? | **Did** Mateo **play** soccer? | ¿Mateo jugó al fútbol? |
| short answer | Yes, he did. / No, he didn't. | Sí. / No. |

**EN:** ★ After *did / didn't*, the verb has **no -ed**: ✗ *I didn't cleaned.* → ✓ *I didn't clean.* And ⚠ *was / were* never use *did*: ✗ *Did you were tired?* → ✓ *Were you tired?*

**ES:** ★ Después de *did / didn't*, el verbo va **sin -ed**: ✗ *I didn't cleaned.* → ✓ *I didn't clean.* Y ⚠ *was / were* nunca usan *did*: ✗ *Did you were tired?* → ✓ *Were you tired?*

### A7 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|-------------------------------|-------------------------|---------------------------------|
| She works yesterday. | She **worked** yesterday. | finished past time → past verb |
| I studyed a lot. | I stud**ied** a lot. | consonant + y → -ied |
| The bus stoped. | The bus sto**pp**ed. | short verb: double the consonant |
| I was work yesterday. | I **worked** yesterday. | don't mix *was* with a normal verb |
| They was tired. | They **were** tired. | you / we / they → were |
| Was cold on Sunday. | **It** was cold on Sunday. | subject seat never empty |
| I cooked yesterday night. | I cooked **last night**. | anoche = last night |
| We arrived ago two days. | We arrived **two days ago**. | *ago* goes after the time |
| I called her in the last week. | I called her **last week**. | no *in*, no *the* before *last* |
| I didn't watched TV. | I didn't **watch** TV. | after *did*, base verb |

### Word tip / Consejo de palabras

**EN:** *Last* has two meanings. Before a time word, it means *pasado/a*: *last week* = *la semana pasada*. With *the*, it means *último/a*: *the last week of May* = *la última semana de mayo*; *the last page* = *la última página*. So: *last Saturday* (el sábado pasado) ≠ *the last Saturday of the month* (el último sábado del mes).

**ES:** *Last* tiene dos significados. Antes de una palabra de tiempo significa *pasado/a*: *last week* = *la semana pasada*. Con *the* significa *último/a*: *the last week of May* = *la última semana de mayo*; *the last page* = *la última página*. Entonces: *last Saturday* (el sábado pasado) ≠ *the last Saturday of the month* (el último sábado del mes).

### A busy weekend

> Last Monday I started my new job at the front desk of the Lakeview Hotel. I worked from 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. every day. The work was interesting, and the guests (los huéspedes) were friendly. But on Friday evening I was very tired!
>
> On Saturday morning I stayed in bed until nine o'clock. Then we walked to the park. It was cold, but it was sunny. Mateo played soccer with Leo, and I talked to Leo's mother. After lunch Mateo and I cleaned the apartment. He washed the dishes, and I cleaned the kitchen and the bathroom.
>
> In the evening I cooked arroz con pollo for Mrs. Park. She loved it. We talked about the hotel, and she listened to all my stories. Tofu, her cat, watched us from the sofa. Then she helped me with my English homework.
>
> On Sunday it snowed, and Mateo and I were at home all day. Mateo called his father in Cali. I called my mother, and we talked for an hour. In the afternoon we watched a movie about dinosaurs. Mateo loved it, of course. I was so tired — I closed my eyes in the middle of the movie!
>
> It was a busy weekend. I didn't relax very much, but I was happy.
