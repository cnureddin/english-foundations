## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** One year ago, Lucía arrived in Chicago. Now people ask her: "Is Chicago better than Cali?" To answer, she needs **comparing words**. Good news: they are adjectives (Unit 7), and they sit in the seats you already know. Only the adjective changes its shape.

**ES:** Hace un año, Lucía llegó a Chicago. Ahora la gente le pregunta: "Is Chicago better than Cali?" (¿Chicago es mejor que Cali?). Para responder, necesita **palabras para comparar**. Buena noticia: son adjetivos (Unidad 7), y se sientan en los asientos que ya conoces. Solo el adjetivo cambia de forma.

### A1 · Where comparing words sit / Dónde se sientan las palabras de comparación

**EN:** A comparing adjective sits where every adjective sits: in the **DESCRIPTION seat** after *be*, or **before a noun**. The first row is the control row: a plain adjective. The seats do not change.

**ES:** Un adjetivo de comparación se sienta donde se sienta todo adjetivo: en el **asiento de DESCRIPCIÓN** después de *be*, o **antes de un sustantivo**. La primera fila es la fila de control: un adjetivo normal. Los asientos no cambian.

| SUBJECT | VERB | DESCRIPTION | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicago | is | big. | Chicago es grande. |
| Chicago | is | **bigger than** Cali. | Chicago es más grande que Cali. |
| Chicago | is | **a bigger** city. | Chicago es una ciudad más grande. |
| Chicago | is | **the biggest** city in Illinois. | Chicago es la ciudad más grande de Illinois. |

**EN:** ★ Spanish uses one method for everything: *más* + adjective (*más grande*, *más caro*). English has **two methods**: a short adjective gets an ending (**-er**), a long adjective gets **more**. And Spanish *que* becomes **than**.

**ES:** ★ El español usa un solo método para todo: *más* + adjetivo (*más grande*, *más caro*). El inglés tiene **dos métodos**: un adjetivo corto recibe una terminación (**-er**), un adjetivo largo recibe **more**. Y el *que* del español se convierte en **than**.

### A2 · Short adjectives: -er + than / Adjetivos cortos: -er + than

**EN:** A **short adjective** has one syllable (*cold, big, nice*). It takes **-er**. Two-syllable adjectives that end in **-y** (*happy, busy, noisy*) also take -er. Watch the spelling:

**ES:** Un **adjetivo corto** tiene una sílaba (*cold, big, nice*). Recibe **-er**. Los adjetivos de dos sílabas que terminan en **-y** (*happy, busy, noisy*) también reciben -er. Cuidado con la ortografía:

| Rule / Regla | Adjective | Comparative | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| most words: + -er | cold · cheap · tall | colder · cheaper · taller | más frío · más barato · más alto |
| ends in -e: + -r | nice · safe · large | nicer · safer · larger | más lindo · más seguro · más grande |
| 1 vowel + 1 consonant: double it | big · hot · wet | bigger · hotter · wetter | más grande · más caliente · más mojado |
| consonant + -y: y → i + -er | happy · busy · noisy | happier · busier · noisier | más feliz · más ocupado · más ruidoso |

⚠ *Quiet* has two syllables, but it takes -er: *quiet → quieter*. And after *w* there is no double letter: *new → newer*, *slow → slower*.

⚠ *Quiet* tiene dos sílabas, pero recibe -er: *quiet → quieter*. Y después de *w* no se duplica la letra: *new → newer*, *slow → slower*.

### A3 · Long adjectives: more + adjective + than / Adjetivos largos: more + adjetivo + than

**EN:** A **long adjective** has two or more syllables and does not end in -y (*expensive, beautiful, crowded, modern*). It does not change. Put **more** in front of it.

**ES:** Un **adjetivo largo** tiene dos sílabas o más y no termina en -y (*expensive, beautiful, crowded, modern*). No cambia. Pon **more** delante.

| SUBJECT | VERB | DESCRIPTION | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rent in Chicago | is | **more expensive than** in Cali. | El alquiler en Chicago es más caro que en Cali. |
| The mountains | are | **more beautiful than** the city. | Las montañas son más bonitas que la ciudad. |
| The L train | is | **more crowded** at 8 a.m. | El tren L está más lleno a las 8 a.m. |

**EN:** ★ One method only — never both. *More* + *-er* together is a very common mistake:

**ES:** ★ Un solo método — nunca los dos. *More* + *-er* juntos es un error muy común:

* ✗ ~~Chicago is more big than Cali.~~ → ✓ Chicago is **bigger** than Cali.
* ✗ ~~Chicago is more bigger than Cali.~~ → ✓ Chicago is **bigger** than Cali.
* ✗ ~~Chicago is expensiver.~~ → ✓ Chicago is **more expensive**.

**⚠ *Que* → *than*, never *that* / *Que* → *than*, nunca *that***

**EN:** Spanish *que* has many English faces. In a comparison, it is always **than**. *That* is a different word.

**ES:** El *que* del español tiene muchas caras en inglés. En una comparación, siempre es **than**. *That* es otra palabra.

* ✗ ~~Mateo is taller that Leo.~~ → ✓ Mateo is taller **than** Leo.

### A4 · The irregular three / Los tres irregulares

**EN:** Three very common adjectives have their own forms. Learn them as a song: *good, better, the best — bad, worse, the worst — far, farther, the farthest.*

**ES:** Tres adjetivos muy comunes tienen sus propias formas. Apréndelos como una canción: *good, better, the best — bad, worse, the worst — far, farther, the farthest.*

| Adjective | Comparative | Superlative | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| good | **better** | **the best** | bueno · mejor · el mejor |
| bad | **worse** | **the worst** | malo · peor · el peor |
| far | **farther** | **the farthest** | lejos · más lejos · el más lejano |

⚠ *Better* already means *más bueno*. Don't add *more*: ✗ ~~more better~~, ✗ ~~more good~~, ✗ ~~gooder~~. The same with *worse*: ✗ ~~more bad~~, ✗ ~~more worse~~.

⚠ *Better* ya significa *mejor*. No añadas *more*: ✗ ~~more better~~, ✗ ~~more good~~, ✗ ~~gooder~~. Lo mismo con *worse*: ✗ ~~more bad~~, ✗ ~~more worse~~.

**Big or small difference? / ¿Diferencia grande o pequeña?**

**EN:** To make the difference bigger, put **much** or **a lot** before the comparative. For a small difference, use **a bit**. ⚠ Not *very*: *very* goes with a plain adjective (*very cold*), not with a comparative.

**ES:** Para hacer la diferencia más grande, pon **much** o **a lot** antes del comparativo. Para una diferencia pequeña, usa **a bit**. ⚠ No *very*: *very* va con un adjetivo normal (*very cold*), no con un comparativo.

* ✓ January in Chicago is **much colder** than January in Cali.
* ✓ The bus is **a bit cheaper** than the train.
* ✗ ~~Chicago is very colder.~~ → ✓ Chicago is **much colder**.

### A5 · Number one: the superlative / El número uno: el superlativo

**EN:** A comparative compares **two** things. A **superlative** says which one is **number one** in a group: *the biggest, the most beautiful*. ★ It always takes **the**.

**ES:** Un comparativo compara **dos** cosas. Un **superlativo** dice cuál es el **número uno** de un grupo: *the biggest, the most beautiful*. ★ Siempre lleva **the**.

| Adjective | Comparative (2 things) | Superlative (number one) | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| cold | colder | **the coldest** | el más frío |
| nice | nicer | **the nicest** | el más lindo |
| big | bigger | **the biggest** | el más grande |
| busy | busier | **the busiest** | el más ocupado |
| beautiful | more beautiful | **the most beautiful** | el más bonito |

**EN:** The superlative lives in the same seats. It can sit in the description seat, or inside a noun group in the subject seat. Look at the control row first:

**ES:** El superlativo vive en los mismos asientos. Puede sentarse en el asiento de descripción, o dentro de un grupo de sustantivo en el asiento del sujeto. Mira primero la fila de control:

| SUBJECT | VERB | DESCRIPTION | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| The problem | is | the cold. | El problema es el frío. |
| **The biggest problem** | is | the cold. | El problema más grande es el frío. |
| Mrs. Park | is | **the kindest** person in our building. | Mrs. Park es la persona más amable de nuestro edificio. |
| This | is | **the most beautiful** beach in Colombia. | Esta es la playa más bonita de Colombia. |

**⚠ Three Spanish traps / Tres trampas del español**

**EN:**

1. **Word order.** Spanish says *la playa más bonita* (noun + más + adjective). In English the adjective comes **before** the noun (Unit 7): ✓ *the most beautiful beach*. ✗ ~~the beach most beautiful~~.
2. **Don't mix the methods.** *El más grande* is ✓ *the biggest*, not ✗ ~~the more big~~ or ✗ ~~the most big~~.
3. **In, not of, for places and groups.** Spanish says *de Colombia*, *de mi familia*. English says ✓ *the biggest city **in** Colombia*, *the tallest person **in** my family*. Use *of* for time: *the coldest day **of** the year*.

**ES:**

1. **Orden de palabras.** El español dice *la playa más bonita* (sustantivo + más + adjetivo). En inglés el adjetivo va **antes** del sustantivo (Unidad 7): ✓ *the most beautiful beach*. ✗ ~~the beach most beautiful~~.
2. **No mezcles los métodos.** *El más grande* es ✓ *the biggest*, no ✗ ~~the more big~~ ni ✗ ~~the most big~~.
3. **In, no of, para lugares y grupos.** El español dice *de Colombia*, *de mi familia*. El inglés dice ✓ *the biggest city **in** Colombia*, *the tallest person **in** my family*. Usa *of* para el tiempo: *the coldest day **of** the year* (el día más frío del año).

### A6 · Equal or not equal: as … as / Igual o no igual: as … as

**EN:** When two things are **the same**, use **as + adjective + as**. The adjective is plain: no -er, no *more*. For "not the same," put **not** after *be*: **not as … as**.

**ES:** Cuando dos cosas son **iguales**, usa **as + adjetivo + as**. El adjetivo va normal: sin -er, sin *more*. Para "no igual", pon **not** después de *be*: **not as … as**.

| SUBJECT | VERB | DESCRIPTION | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| In July, Chicago | is | **as hot as** Cali. | En julio, Chicago es tan caluroso como Cali. |
| The lake | is | **as big as** a sea. | El lago es tan grande como un mar. |
| Cali | is | **not as cold as** Chicago. | Cali no es tan fría como Chicago. |
| My English | is not | **as good as** Mateo's English. | Mi inglés no es tan bueno como el inglés de Mateo. |

⚠ Spanish *tan … como* → English *as … as*. Both words are **as**: ✗ ~~so hot as~~, ✗ ~~as hot than~~, ✗ ~~as hotter as~~.

⚠ *Tan … como* del español → *as … as* en inglés. Las dos palabras son **as**: ✗ ~~so hot as~~, ✗ ~~as hot than~~, ✗ ~~as hotter as~~.

**Mini-practice 4 / Mini-práctica 4** — Rewrite with *not as … as*. Keep the meaning. / Reescribe con *not as … as*. Mantén el sentido.

2. The train is faster than the bus. → The bus ___

### A7 · After than: me or I am? / Después de than: ¿me o I am?

**EN:** After *than* (and after *as … as*), use an **object pronoun** — *me, him, her, us, them* (Unit 4). Or use a full engine: **subject + verb**. Both are correct.

**ES:** Después de *than* (y después de *as … as*), usa un **pronombre de objeto** — *me, him, her, us, them* (Unidad 4). O usa un motor completo: **sujeto + verbo**. Las dos formas son correctas.

| Object pronoun (everyday) | Full engine | Español |
|---|---|---|
| Ana is older than **me**. | Ana is older than **I am**. | Ana es mayor que yo. |
| Carlos is taller than **her**. | Carlos is taller than **she is**. | Carlos es más alto que ella. |
| Mateo runs faster than **us**. | Mateo runs faster than **we do**. | Mateo corre más rápido que nosotros. |

⚠ A contraction cannot end a sentence: ✗ *Ana is older than I'm.* → ✓ *than I am*.

⚠ Una contracción no puede terminar una oración: ✗ *Ana is older than I'm.* → ✓ *than I am*.

### A8 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| Chicago is more big than Cali. | Chicago is **bigger** than Cali. | short adjective → -er, not *more* |
| This is more better. | This is **better**. | *better* already means *más bueno* |
| Rent is expensiver here. | Rent is **more expensive** here. | long adjective → *more* |
| Mateo is taller that Leo. | Mateo is taller **than** Leo. | comparison *que* = *than* |
| She is most kind person. | She is **the kindest** person. | superlative → *the* + -est |
| the city most big of Colombia | **the biggest city in** Colombia | adjective before noun; *in* for places |
| Cali is so warm as Miami. | Cali is **as** warm **as** Miami. | *tan … como* = *as … as* |
| It is very colder today. | It is **much** colder today. | *much / a lot / a bit* + comparative |
| The bus is more cheap. | The bus is **cheaper**. | *cheap* is short → -er |
| The weather is badder today. | The weather is **worse** today. | *bad → worse → the worst* |

### Word tip / Consejo de palabras

**EN:** Don't confuse **quiet** and **quite**. *Quiet* (two sounds: "kuái-et") means *tranquilo*: *a quiet street*. *Quite* (one sound: "kuáit") means *bastante* (Unit 7): *quite cold*. And in the United States, temperature is in **Fahrenheit**: 32 °F = 0 °C, and 90 °F = about 32 °C. So "It's 90 degrees!" means it is very hot.

**ES:** No confundas **quiet** y **quite**. *Quiet* (dos sonidos: "kuái-et") significa *tranquilo*: *a quiet street*. *Quite* (un sonido: "kuáit") significa *bastante* (Unidad 7): *quite cold*. Y en Estados Unidos la temperatura se mide en **Fahrenheit**: 32 °F = 0 °C, y 90 °F = unos 32 °C. Así que "It's 90 degrees!" significa que hace mucho calor.

> *Ana writes a blog. This September, she asked Lucía to write a post for it.*

> One year ago, Mateo and I arrived in Chicago with two suitcases. Last week my friend Ana asked me a question for her blog: "Which city is better, Chicago or Cali?" It is not an easy question!
>
> First, the weather. Cali is warm all year. In July, Chicago is almost as hot as Cali, and it is more humid. But in January, Chicago is much colder. Last winter was the coldest winter of my life! For me, the weather in Cali is better.
>
> Next, the size. Chicago is bigger than Cali, and its buildings are much taller. Downtown is noisier and more crowded, but our street is quiet. Cali has beautiful green mountains around it. Chicago is flat, but it has Lake Michigan. The lake is as big as a sea! Walking by the lake is my favorite thing here.
>
> What about the people? People in Cali talk to you on the bus, and they dance at every party. People in Chicago are quieter, but they are very kind. My neighbor, Mrs. Park, is the kindest person in our building.
>

>
> Prices are a problem. Chicago is more expensive than Cali. Rent, bus tickets, and a cup of coffee cost more here. But my salary at the Lakeview Hotel is higher, too.
>
> So which city is better? Cali is warmer and cheaper, and my mother and my brother are there. Chicago is more modern, and my job and Mateo's school are here. My English is better than last year, but it is not as good as Mateo's! Cali is my first home. Now Chicago is my home, too. Maybe the best city is the city with the people you love.
