## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** In Unit 10 every verb took **-ed**. But the most common English verbs don't follow that rule: *go → went, see → saw, buy → bought*. This unit gives you the **30 irregular verbs** you need most. Then it completes the *did* machine for negatives and questions. At the end, two reading skills help you understand new words without a dictionary.

**ES:** En la Unidad 10 todos los verbos llevaban **-ed**. Pero los verbos más comunes del inglés no siguen esa regla: *go → went, see → saw, buy → bought*. Esta unidad te da los **30 verbos irregulares** que más necesitas. Después completa la máquina de *did* para negaciones y preguntas. Al final, dos destrezas de lectura te ayudan a entender palabras nuevas sin diccionario.

### A1 · Irregular verbs: the memory list / Verbos irregulares: la lista de memoria

**EN:** Irregular verbs refuse the -ed and change shape. There is no rule — only memory. But there is good news: (1) like regular verbs, the past form is the **same for every subject** (*I went, she went, they went*); (2) these 30 verbs cover most of what you say every day. The groups below help your memory: verbs in the same group change in a similar way.

**ES:** Los verbos irregulares rechazan el -ed y cambian de forma. No hay regla — solo memoria. Pero hay buenas noticias: (1) como los regulares, el pasado es **igual para todos los sujetos** (*I went, she went, they went*); (2) estos 30 verbos cubren casi todo lo que dices cada día. Los grupos de abajo ayudan a tu memoria: los verbos del mismo grupo cambian de forma parecida.

| base → past | Español | Example / Ejemplo |
|------------------------------|-----------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **1 · No change / Sin cambio** | | |
| put → **put** | poner | I put the keys on the table. |
| read → **read** (sounds "red") | leer | Mateo read a book about dinosaurs. |
| **2 · -ought / -aught (same sound / mismo sonido)** | | |
| think → **thought** | pensar | I thought the class was at 7. |
| buy → **bought** | comprar | Lucía bought a warm coat. |
| bring → **brought** | traer | Ana brought cookies to class. |
| teach → **taught** | enseñar | Mrs. Park taught math. |

| drink → **drank** | beber, tomar | We drank coffee. |

**EN:** How to learn them: **5 verbs a day**. Say the pair aloud (*go – went, go – went*), then write one sentence about yourself. Put the verbs you forget on a card. The full list of irregular verbs is in **Appendix A** at the end of the book.

**ES:** Cómo aprenderlos: **5 verbos por día**. Di el par en voz alta (*go – went, go – went*) y escribe una oración sobre ti. Pon en una tarjeta los verbos que olvidas. La lista completa de verbos irregulares está en el **Apéndice A** al final del libro.

**⚠ No -ed on an irregular verb / Nada de -ed en un verbo irregular**

**EN:** Your brain knows the -ed rule now, so it wants to use it everywhere: ✗ *goed*, ✗ *buyed*, ✗ *teached*. Mateo hears this from his mother and laughs: "Mom, not *teached* — **taught**!"

**ES:** Tu cerebro ya conoce la regla del -ed, así que quiere usarla en todas partes: ✗ *goed*, ✗ *buyed*, ✗ *teached*. Mateo se lo escucha a su mamá y se ríe: "Mamá, no *teached* — **taught**!"

### A2 · Negatives: didn't + base verb / Negaciones: didn't + verbo base

**EN:** You saw *did* in Unit 10. Now here is the full machine. Remember Unit 5: in *She doesn't work*, the -s jumps from the main verb to the helper. The past works the same way: the past **jumps off** the main verb and onto **did**. The main verb goes back to its base form. *Did* is the same for every subject.

**ES:** Viste *did* en la Unidad 10. Aquí está la máquina completa. Recuerda la Unidad 5: en *She doesn't work*, la -s salta del verbo principal al auxiliar. El pasado funciona igual: el pasado **salta** del verbo principal al auxiliar **did**. El verbo principal vuelve a su forma base. *Did* es igual para todos los sujetos.

| SUBJECT / SUJETO | VERB (helper + main) | OBJECT / REST | Español |
|-----------------|----------------------|--------------|-------------------------------------|
| She | went | home. | Se fue a casa. |
| She | **didn't go** | home. | No se fue a casa. |
| I | **didn't eat** | breakfast. | No desayuné. |
| We | **didn't see** | the movie. | No vimos la película. |
| Mrs. Park | **didn't speak** | much English. | Mrs. Park no hablaba mucho inglés. |

**★ The double-past crime / El crimen del doble pasado**

**EN:** One past per sentence — and *did* owns it. ✗ *I didn't went.* ✗ *She didn't bought it.* → ✓ *I didn't go. She didn't buy it.*

**ES:** Un solo pasado por oración — y *did* es el dueño. ✗ *I didn't went.* ✗ *She didn't bought it.* → ✓ *I didn't go. She didn't buy it.*

**⚠ "No + verb" is Spanish / "No + verbo" es español**

**EN:** In Spanish, *no* goes before the verb: *No vino.* English needs the helper: ✗ *She no came.* → ✓ *She **didn't come**.*

**ES:** En español, *no* va antes del verbo: *No vino.* El inglés necesita el auxiliar: ✗ *She no came.* → ✓ *She **didn't come**.*

### A3 · Questions: did + subject + base / Preguntas: did + sujeto + base

**EN:** Questions use the question machine from Unit 9: **Question word + Auxiliary + Subject + Main verb**. In the past, the auxiliary is **did**. The main verb is base, as in negatives.

**ES:** Las preguntas usan la máquina de preguntas de la Unidad 9: **palabra interrogativa + Auxiliar + Sujeto + verbo principal**. En pasado, el auxiliar es **did**. El verbo principal va en base, como en las negaciones.

| Question word | Auxiliary | Subject | Main verb | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| — | Did | you | sleep | well? |
| — | Did | Mrs. Park | teach | math? |
| Where | did | she | grow up? | — |
| When | did | they | come | to Chicago? |
| What | did | you | eat | for lunch? |

| Question / Pregunta | Short answer / Respuesta corta | Español |
|--------------------------|-------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Did you sleep well? | Yes, I did. / No, I didn't. | ¿Dormiste bien? |
| Did Mrs. Park teach math? | Yes, she did. | ¿Mrs. Park enseñaba matemáticas? |
| Where did she grow up? | In Seoul. | ¿Dónde creció? |

**⚠ Who came? — no *did* / ¿Quién vino? — sin *did***

**EN:** Remember Unit 9: when *who* or *what* is the **subject**, there is no helper. ✗ *Who did come?* → ✓ *Who came to the party?* ✗ *What did happen?* → ✓ *What happened?*

**ES:** Recuerda la Unidad 9: cuando *who* o *what* es el **sujeto**, no hay auxiliar. ✗ *Who did come?* → ✓ *Who came to the party?* ✗ *What did happen?* → ✓ *What happened?*

### A4 · *Was/were* or *did*? The fork again / ¿*Was/were* o *did*? La bifurcación otra vez

**EN:** In Unit 5 you learned the fork: *be* has its own machine; normal verbs use *do*. The past has the same fork. *Be* flips and adds *not*. Normal verbs use *did*.

**ES:** En la Unidad 5 aprendiste la bifurcación: *be* tiene su propia máquina; los verbos normales usan *do*. El pasado tiene la misma bifurcación. *Be* se invierte y agrega *not*. Los verbos normales usan *did*.

| | *be* (was / were) | normal verbs (did) |
|---|---|---|
| + | She was at home. | She stayed at home. |
| − | She wasn't at home. | She didn't stay at home. |
| ? | Was she at home? | Did she stay at home? |

**EN:** ★ Never *did* + *was/were*: ✗ *Did she was sick?* → ✓ *Was she sick?* ✗ *I didn't was tired.* → ✓ *I wasn't tired.*

**ES:** ★ Nunca *did* + *was/were*: ✗ *Did she was sick?* → ✓ *Was she sick?* ✗ *I didn't was tired.* → ✓ *I wasn't tired.*

**⚠ *Nacer* = be born**

**EN:** In English, *nacer* is **be born**, so it uses the *be* machine: *I **was** born in Cali. Where **were** you born? When **was** Mateo born?* ✗ *I born in Cali.* ✗ *Where did you born?*

**ES:** En inglés, *nacer* es **be born**, así que usa la máquina de *be*: *I **was** born in Cali* (Nací en Cali). *Where **were** you born?* (¿Dónde naciste?) ✗ *I born in Cali.* ✗ *Where did you born?*

### A5 · Reading skill: context clues / Destreza de lectura: pistas de contexto

**EN:** When you read, you always meet new words. **Don't stop and translate every one.** First, ask the sentence. Writers often leave a clue near the new word. Here are four kinds of clues.

**ES:** Cuando lees, siempre encuentras palabras nuevas. **No te detengas a traducir cada una.** Primero, pregúntale a la oración. Los escritores muchas veces dejan una pista cerca de la palabra nueva. Aquí tienes cuatro tipos de pistas.

| Clue / Pista | Look for / Busca | Example | Meaning |
|-----------------------|--------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|-------------------|
| Definition / definición | *is, means,* a dash, a colon | *A **landlord** is the owner of an apartment.* | dueño |
| Example / ejemplo | *such as, like, for example* | *I bought **groceries**, such as milk, eggs and bread.* | food, things to eat |
| Contrast / contraste | *but, not* | *Leo is **noisy**, but Mateo is quiet.* | not quiet |
| Logic / lógica | the situation | *After 12 hours at work, Lucía was **exhausted**.* | very tired |

**The 4 steps / Los 4 pasos**

**EN:** (1) Read the whole sentence. (2) Ask: which seat is the word in — is it a noun, a verb, an adjective? (Unit 1.) (3) Look for a clue. (4) Guess, and read on. Use the dictionary only if the word comes back and you still don't understand.

**ES:** (1) Lee la oración completa. (2) Pregunta: ¿en qué asiento está la palabra — es sustantivo, verbo, adjetivo? (Unidad 1.) (3) Busca una pista. (4) Adivina y sigue leyendo. Usa el diccionario solo si la palabra vuelve a aparecer y todavía no entiendes.

**Mini-practice 5 / Mini-práctica 5** — What does the **bold** word mean? What kind of clue helped you? / ¿Qué significa la palabra en **negrita**? ¿Qué tipo de pista te ayudó?

1. *A **nap** is a short sleep in the afternoon.*
2. *The first day was **awful**, but the second day was wonderful.*
3. *Mateo didn't eat all day. At 6 p.m. he was **starving**.*

### A6 · Reading skill: Spanish cousins and false friends / Destreza de lectura: primos del español y falsos amigos

**EN:** Here is your superpower: many English words come from Latin, like Spanish. When an English word looks like a Spanish word, it is usually its **cousin** (a *cognate*) and means the same. Word endings help you find them.

**ES:** Aquí está tu superpoder: muchas palabras inglesas vienen del latín, como el español. Cuando una palabra inglesa se parece a una española, normalmente es su **prima** (un *cognado*) y significa lo mismo. Las terminaciones te ayudan a encontrarlas.

| English ending | Spanish ending | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -tion | -ción | information, vacation, station |
| -ty | -dad | university, city, activity |
| -ly | -mente | finally, really, normally |
| -ous | -oso | famous, delicious, nervous |
| -ic | -ico | music, public, fantastic |

**EN:** Two small word parts help too: **un-** = not (*unhappy, unfriendly*) and **re-** = again (*rewrite, reread*).

**ES:** Dos partes pequeñas también ayudan: **un-** = no (*unhappy* = infeliz, *unfriendly* = antipático) y **re-** = otra vez (*rewrite* = reescribir, *reread* = releer).

**⚠ False friends: the cousins who lie / Falsos amigos: los primos que mienten**

| English | Really means / Significa | NOT / NO es |
|---|---|---|
| actually | en realidad | actualmente (= now, currently) |
| library | biblioteca | librería (= bookstore) |
| parents | padres (mamá y papá) | parientes (= relatives) |
| embarrassed | avergonzado/a | embarazada (= pregnant) |
| exit | salida | éxito (= success) |
| assist | ayudar | asistir a (= attend) |

**Mini-practice 6 / Mini-práctica 6** — What does the **bold** word mean in Spanish? / ¿Qué significa la palabra en **negrita** en español?

1. *Mrs. Park's **parents** lived in Seoul.*
2. *Mateo reads about dinosaurs at the **library**.*
3. *Chicago is **famous** for its pizza.*

### A7 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|-----------------------|-----------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| I goed to the store. | I **went** to the store. | irregular verb — memory list |
| She teached math. | She **taught** math. | irregular verb — memory list |
| I readed a book. | I **read** a book. | same spelling, sounds "red" |
| I didn't went. | I didn't **go**. | *did* carries the past; main verb = base |
| Did you saw Leo? | Did you **see** Leo? | same law |
| She no came. | She **didn't come**. | "no + verb" is Spanish; use the helper |
| Did she was sick? | **Was she** sick? | *be* never uses *did* — flip it |
| I born in Cali. | I **was born** in Cali. | *nacer* = *be born* |
| What did happen? | What **happened**? | *what* is the subject: no *did* |

### Word tip / Consejo de palabras

**EN:** Spanish *conocer* has two English friends. **Meet** = the first time: *I met Ana in September* (conocí a Ana). **Know** = you already know the person: *I know Ana very well* (conozco muy bien a Ana). So ✗ *I knew my husband at university* → ✓ *I **met** my husband at university.*

**ES:** El verbo *conocer* tiene dos amigos en inglés. **Meet** = la primera vez: *I met Ana in September* (conocí a Ana en septiembre). **Know** = ya conoces a la persona: *I know Ana very well* (conozco muy bien a Ana). Entonces ✗ *I knew my husband at university* → ✓ *I **met** my husband at university* (conocí a mi esposo en la universidad).

### Mrs. Park's story

*For my English class, I asked my neighbor about her life. — Lucía*

> Helen Park was born in Seoul, South Korea, in 1953. She grew up in a small apartment with her parents and her two brothers. Her family didn't have much money, but her parents bought a lot of books for their children. As a child, Helen loved numbers.
>
> At eighteen, Helen went to university in Seoul. She studied mathematics. There she met a young engineering student, Min-ho Park. They got married in 1974.
>
> In 1975 Min-ho got a job in Chicago, and they came to the United States. The first year was difficult. Helen was homesick: she missed her family and her country every day. She knew some English from school, but she didn't understand people in the stores. She took English classes at night — like me! She also read a lot at the library.
>
> Later, Helen taught math at a high school in Chicago for 35 years. She was a strict teacher: nobody talked in her class, and everybody did the homework. But her students loved her. She retired in 2010.
>
> Min-ho died in 2019. Now Mrs. Park lives alone with her cat, Tofu. Her English is fluent today — she speaks it perfectly — but she still speaks Korean on the phone with her brothers.
>
> At the end, I asked, "Was it a good life?" She thought for a moment. Then she said, "I left my home, but I found a new one."
