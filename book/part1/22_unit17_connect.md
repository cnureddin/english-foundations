## A · Grammar / Gramática

**EN:** Until now, most of your sentences were short: one subject, one verb. Real English joins ideas: *Lucía was tired, **but** she went to class.* This unit teaches the legal ways to join two ideas in one sentence — and the three ways to break a sentence. At the end, you learn to find the true subject, so the verb always matches.

**ES:** Hasta ahora, la mayoría de tus oraciones eran cortas: un sujeto, un verbo. El inglés real une ideas: *Lucía was tired, **but** she went to class* (Lucía estaba cansada, pero fue a clase). Esta unidad enseña las formas legales de unir dos ideas en una oración — y las tres formas de romper una oración. Al final, aprendes a encontrar el verdadero sujeto, para que el verbo siempre concuerde.

### A1 · One engine = one clause / Un motor = una cláusula

**EN:** A **clause** is one subject + one verb: **one engine**. A **simple sentence** has one engine. But one engine can be **wide**: the subject seat can hold a **team** (*Ana and Lucía*), and the verb seat can hold **two actions** for the same subject (*cooks and cleans*). That *and* inside one seat does **not** make a second engine.

**ES:** Una **cláusula** es un sujeto + un verbo: **un motor**. Una **oración simple** tiene un motor. Pero un motor puede ser **ancho**: el asiento del sujeto puede tener un **equipo** (*Ana and Lucía*), y el asiento del verbo puede tener **dos acciones** para el mismo sujeto (*cooks and cleans*). Ese *and* dentro de un asiento **no** crea un segundo motor.

| SUBJECT | VERB | rest | Engines / Motores |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lucía | works | at the hotel. | 1 (simple) |
| **Lucía and Ana** | study | together. | 1 — a team subject |
| Lucía | **cooks and cleans** | on Saturday. | 1 — two actions, one subject |
| Lucía works, | and Ana | studies. | 2 — two subjects, two verbs |

★ **The master skill:** to understand any long sentence, first **count the engines** — count the subject + verb pairs.

★ **La habilidad maestra:** para entender cualquier oración larga, primero **cuenta los motores** — cuenta los pares sujeto + verbo.

**Mini-practice 1 / Mini-práctica 1** — How many engines? / ¿Cuántos motores?

1. Mateo and Leo play soccer after school.
2. I work at the hotel, and my son goes to school.
3. Mrs. Park reads and cooks every evening.
4. The exam was hard, but I passed.

### A2 · Two equal engines: and, but, or, so / Dos motores iguales: and, but, or, so

**EN:** A **compound sentence** joins two complete sentences as **equals**. The glue is a **comma + a joining word**. English has seven joining words (teachers call them **FANBOYS**: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*), but at A2 you need four:

**ES:** Una **oración compuesta** une dos oraciones completas como **iguales**. El pegamento es una **coma + una palabra de unión**. El inglés tiene siete palabras de unión (los profesores las llaman **FANBOYS**: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*), pero en A2 necesitas cuatro:

| Joiner | Job / Trabajo | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| **and** | + one more idea | I work in the day, **and** I study at night. | y |
| **but** | contrast | The job is stressful, **but** I like it. | pero |
| **or** | choice | We can take the bus, **or** we can walk. | o |
| **so** | → result | I was tired, **so** I went to bed early. | así que, por eso |

**Pattern / Patrón:** `SENTENCE 1 + , + and / but / or / so + SENTENCE 2`

**EN:** The comma goes **before** the joining word, and only when there are **two engines**. With one wide engine (two actions, one subject), there is no comma: *Lucía cooks and cleans.*

**ES:** La coma va **antes** de la palabra de unión, y solo cuando hay **dos motores**. Con un motor ancho (dos acciones, un sujeto), no hay coma: *Lucía cooks and cleans.*

**Mini-practice 2 / Mini-práctica 2** — Join with *and, but, or* or *so*. Add the comma. / Une con *and, but, or* o *so*. Añade la coma.

1. It was cold. We walked by the lake.
2. The hotel was full. Everyone was busy.
3. I missed the bus. I was late for work.
4. You can call me. You can send an email.

### A3 · A boss and a helper: because, when, before, after / Un jefe y un ayudante

**EN:** A **complex sentence** also has two engines, but they are not equals. One clause is the **boss**: it can live alone. The other is a **helper**: it starts with a word like **because, when, before, after** (or **if**). With that word in front, the helper **cannot live alone**. *I was tired* is a sentence. *Because I was tired* is not — it is waiting for a boss.

**ES:** Una **oración compleja** también tiene dos motores, pero no son iguales. Una cláusula es el **jefe**: puede vivir sola. La otra es un **ayudante**: empieza con una palabra como **because, when, before, after** (o **if**). Con esa palabra delante, el ayudante **no puede vivir solo**. *I was tired* es una oración. *Because I was tired* no lo es — está esperando a un jefe.

| Word | Español | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **because** | porque | I study at night **because** I work in the day. |
| **when** | cuando | **When** the guests arrived, I gave them their keys. |
| **before** | antes de (que) | I have dinner **before** my class starts. |
| **after** | después de (que) | **After** I finish work, I pick up Mateo. |
| **if** *(preview)* | si | **If** I pass the exam, I get a certificate. |

**★ The comma rule is about order / La regla de la coma es sobre el orden**

* Helper **first** → comma after it: ***Because I was tired,** I went to bed early.*
* Boss **first** → no comma: *I went to bed early **because I was tired**.*

★ Good news: Spanish builds these sentences the same way (*porque, cuando, antes de que, si*). Your instinct works here. / Buena noticia: el español construye estas oraciones igual. Tu instinto funciona aquí.

⚠ **Because or so? / ¿Because o so?** They point in opposite directions. *Because* introduces the **cause**; *so* introduces the **result**.

⚠ **¿Because o so?** Apuntan en direcciones opuestas. *Because* introduce la **causa**; *so* introduce el **resultado**.

* *I stayed home **because** I was sick.* (cause → sick)
* *I was sick, **so** I stayed home.* (result → stayed home)

⚠ ***Because* is not *why*.** Spanish *porque* (answer) and *¿por qué?* (question) look almost the same. English uses two different words: ***Why** are you tired? — **Because** I worked all day.*

⚠ ***Because* no es *why*.** *Porque* (respuesta) y *¿por qué?* (pregunta) se parecen mucho en español. El inglés usa dos palabras distintas: ***Why** are you tired? — **Because** I worked all day.*

**EN:** *If* is only a preview here: use it with the simple present in both parts, for things that are always true. You will study *if* sentences in Part 2.

**ES:** *If* aquí es solo un adelanto: úsalo con el presente simple en las dos partes, para cosas que siempre son verdad. Estudiarás las oraciones con *if* en la Parte 2.

**Mini-practice 3 / Mini-práctica 3** — Comma or no comma? Write the sentence. / ¿Coma o no? Escribe la oración.

1. When I got home I made dinner.
2. I made dinner when I got home.
3. Before the class starts I read my notes.
4. Mateo was happy because he won the game.

### A4 · The three crimes / Los tres crímenes

**EN:** All three crimes come from counting the engines wrong. They are very common in writing — and very easy to fix.

**ES:** Los tres crímenes vienen de contar mal los motores. Son muy comunes al escribir — y muy fáciles de corregir.

| Crime / Crimen | What it is / Qué es | ✗ | ✓ Fix / Corrección |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Run-on** | 2 engines, no glue / sin pegamento | It was late we went home. | It was late, **so** we went home. |
| **Comma splice** | 2 engines, only a comma / solo coma | It was late, we went home. | It was late**.** We went home. |
| **Fragment** | a helper with no boss / sin jefe | Because I was tired. | Because I was tired, **I went to bed.** |

⚠ **The Spanish trap / La trampa del español**

**EN:** In Spanish, a comma between two sentences is normal: *"Era tarde, nos fuimos a casa."* **In English it is always a mistake** (a comma splice). When you feel that comma coming, choose from the **fix menu**:

1. a full stop — two sentences: *It was late. We went home.*
2. comma + *and / but / or / so*: *It was late, so we went home.*
3. a helper word: *Because it was late, we went home.*

**ES:** En español, una coma entre dos oraciones es normal: *"Era tarde, nos fuimos a casa."* **En inglés siempre es un error** (un *comma splice*). Cuando sientas venir esa coma, elige del **menú de correcciones**:

1. un punto — dos oraciones: *It was late. We went home.*
2. coma + *and / but / or / so*: *It was late, so we went home.*
3. una palabra ayudante: *Because it was late, we went home.*

**Mini-practice 4 / Mini-práctica 4** — Name the crime: run-on, comma splice or fragment. / Nombra el crimen.

1. When the phone rang.
2. The guest was angry, I called the manager.
3. I love my job I don't love the traffic.

### A5 · Agreement: find the true boss / Concordancia: encuentra al verdadero jefe

**EN:** **Agreement** means the verb matches its subject: *he works*, *they work*, *it is*, *they are*. Spanish does this much more than English (*hablo, hablas, habla…*), so the grammar is easy for you. The difficult part is **finding the true subject** when the sentence hides it. ★ First find the boss, then match the verb. Never match the verb to the nearest noun.

**ES:** **Concordancia** significa que el verbo coincide con su sujeto: *he works*, *they work*, *it is*, *they are*. El español hace esto mucho más que el inglés (*hablo, hablas, habla…*), así que la gramática es fácil para ti. Lo difícil es **encontrar el verdadero sujeto** cuando la oración lo esconde. ★ Primero encuentra al jefe, luego haz coincidir el verbo. Nunca hagas coincidir el verbo con el sustantivo más cercano.

**Rule 1 · The blindfold / La venda.** A phrase like *of the guests, in my class, on the table* (preposition + noun) never holds the boss. Put it in ( brackets ) and it disappears. / Una frase como *of the guests, in my class* (preposición + sustantivo) nunca tiene al jefe. Ponla entre ( paréntesis ) y desaparece.

| SUBJECT (with the blindfold) | VERB | rest | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| The list | is | long. | La lista es larga. |
| The list (of guests) | **is** | long. | La lista de huéspedes es larga. |
| The students (in my class) | **are** | from eight countries. | Los estudiantes de mi clase son de ocho países. |
| One (of my colleagues) | **lives** | on Maple Street. | Uno de mis colegas vive en Maple Street. |

**Rule 2 · Teams with *and* are plural / Los equipos con *and* son plurales.** *Ana and Lucía* = *they* → no -s: *Ana and Lucía **study** together.* ✗ ~~Ana and Lucía studies~~.

**Rule 3 · *Everyone, everybody, each* are singular / son singulares.** They feel plural, but they are *he/she/it*: *Everyone **wants** a room with a view. Each student **has** a book.*

**Rule 4 · ⚠ *People* is plural / *People* es plural.** Spanish says *la gente **es*** (singular). English says *people **are*** (plural): *People in Chicago **are** kind.* ✗ ~~People is kind~~.

**Rule 5 · *There is / there are*: the boss comes after the verb / el jefe viene después.** *There* only holds the seat (Unit 3). Look **after** the verb: *There **is** a problem. There **are** two problems.*

**Mini-practice 5 / Mini-práctica 5** — Choose the verb. / Elige el verbo.

1. The keys to the room (is / are) at the front desk.
2. Everybody in my family (loves / love) soccer.
3. Mateo and Leo (is / are) in fifth grade.
4. There (is / are) many guests this week.
5. People at the hotel (is / are) very friendly.

### A6 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ✗ | ✓ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I was tired, I went to bed. | I was tired, **so** I went to bed. | comma splice: a comma is not glue |
| I love my job I work a lot. | I love my job**, and** I work a lot. | run-on: two engines need glue |
| Because the bus was late. | Because the bus was late, **I was late.** | fragment: the helper needs a boss |
| I went to bed early, because I was tired. | I went to bed early because I was tired. | boss first → no comma |
| I was sick, because I stayed home. | I was sick, **so** I stayed home. | *so* = result, *because* = cause |
| The list of guests are long. | The list (of guests) **is** long. | the boss is *list* |
| Ana and Lucía studies together. | Ana and Lucía **study** together. | team with *and* → plural |
| Everyone know the answer. | Everyone **knows** the answer. | *everyone* = he/she/it |
| People is very kind here. | People **are** very kind here. | *people* is plural |
| There is two problems. | There **are** two problems. | the boss comes after the verb |
