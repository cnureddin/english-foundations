# Foundation Sheet 2 · Simple Present & the *do/does* Machine
# Hoja de Fundamentos 2 · Presente Simple y la Máquina de *do/does*

**Prerequisites / Prerrequisitos:** FS-0 (seats, helper verbs), FS-1 (*be*).

---

## Part 1 · What the simple present is for / Para qué sirve el presente simple

**EN:** The simple present does **not** mean "now." It describes things that are **generally true**: habits and routines (*I drink coffee every morning*), facts (*Water boils at 100°C*), and permanent situations (*She works in a hospital*). For something happening at this moment, English uses a different tense (FS-3). Signal words that point to simple present: *always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week.*

**ES:** El presente simple **no** significa "ahora." Describe cosas que son **generalmente verdad**: hábitos y rutinas (*I drink coffee every morning* — tomo café cada mañana), hechos (*Water boils at 100°C*), y situaciones permanentes (*She works in a hospital*). Para algo que pasa en este momento, el inglés usa otro tiempo (FS-3). Palabras señal del presente simple: *always, usually, often, sometimes, never, every day/week.*

## Part 2 · The form and the famous -s / La forma y la famosa -s

**EN:** The verb stays in its base form for everyone — **except** *he / she / it*, which add **-s**. This tiny -s is the #1 forgotten letter in learner English. English lost almost all its verb endings centuries ago; this -s is the survivor, and teachers always check for it.

**ES:** El verbo queda en su forma base para todos — **excepto** *he / she / it*, que añaden **-s**. Esta pequeña -s es la letra más olvidada por los estudiantes. El inglés perdió casi todas sus terminaciones verbales hace siglos; esta -s es la sobreviviente, y los profesores siempre la revisan.

| SUBJECT | VERB | OBJECT | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | work | at a bank. | trabajo/trabajas/trabajamos/trabajan en un banco |
| He / She / It / Maria | work**s** | at a bank. | trabaja en un banco |

Spelling of the -s / Ortografía de la -s: most verbs **+s** (works, eats) · after -o, -ch, -sh, -ss, -x **+es** (goes, watches, washes) · consonant + y → **-ies** (study → studies; but play → plays) · special: have → **has**.

### Mini-practice 1: 1. She (live) ___ in Bogotá. 2. They (play) ___ soccer. 3. He (watch) ___ TV at night. 4. My brother (have) ___ a dog.

## Part 3 · The big idea: an empty helper carries the grammar / La gran idea: un auxiliar vacío lleva la gramática

**EN:** Now the strangest — and most important — machine in English. To make a **negative** or a **question** with a normal verb, English does not touch the main verb. Instead, it brings in a helper with no meaning of its own: **do / does**. The helper's only job is to **carry the grammar** — the tense and that third-person -s — so the main verb can relax back into its **base form**.

Think of it like this: the -s **jumps off the main verb and onto the helper**.

* *She work**s**.* → the -s lives on *work*.
* *She **does** not work.* → the -s moved into *does*; *work* is base again.
* ***Does** she work?* → same: the helper carries it, in front of the subject.

Spanish has **nothing** like this. In Spanish, negation is just *no* + verb (*no trabaja*) and questions are intonation (*¿trabaja?*). This is why do-support feels alien — and why it needs its own machine in your head.

**ES:** Ahora la máquina más extraña — y más importante — del inglés. Para hacer una **negación** o una **pregunta** con un verbo normal, el inglés no toca el verbo principal. En su lugar, trae un auxiliar sin significado propio: **do / does**. El único trabajo del auxiliar es **llevar la gramática** — el tiempo y esa -s de tercera persona — para que el verbo principal vuelva a su **forma base**.

Piénsalo así: la -s **salta del verbo principal al auxiliar**.

* *She work**s**.* → la -s vive en *work*.
* *She **does** not work.* → la -s se mudó a *does*; *work* vuelve a la base.
* ***Does** she work?* → igual: el auxiliar la lleva, delante del sujeto.

El español **no tiene nada** parecido. En español, la negación es solo *no* + verbo (*no trabaja*) y las preguntas son entonación (*¿trabaja?*). Por eso el do-support se siente tan raro — y por eso necesita su propia máquina en tu cabeza.

## Part 4 · The machine, assembled / La máquina, armada

**Negatives / Negaciones:** SUBJECT + **don't / doesn't** + BASE verb

| SUBJECT | HELPER + not | MAIN VERB (base!) | rest | Español |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | don't | drink | coffee. | no tomo/tomas/... café |
| He / She / It | **doesn't** | drink | coffee. | no toma café |

**Questions / Preguntas:** **Do / Does** + SUBJECT + BASE verb

| HELPER | SUBJECT | MAIN VERB (base!) | rest | Español |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Do | you | like | tea? | ¿Te gusta el té? |
| **Does** | she | like | tea? | ¿Le gusta el té? |

Short answers use only the helper / Las respuestas cortas usan solo el auxiliar: *Does she work here? — Yes, she **does**. / No, she **doesn't**.* (Never repeat the main verb: ✗ *Yes, she works here* is possible but the short form is *Yes, she does.*)

⚠️ **The double -s crime / El crimen de la doble -s:** once *does/doesn't* is in the sentence, the main verb must be BASE. ✗ *Does she work**s**?* ✗ *She doesn't work**s**.* → ✅ *Does she work? She doesn't work.* One -s per sentence, and the helper owns it.

## Part 5 · *be* vs. everything else / *be* contra todos los demás

**EN:** This is the fork in the road for the whole language. Before making any negative or question, ask: **is the verb *be*, or a normal verb?**

| | verb = *be* (FS-1) | normal verb (this sheet) |
|---|---|---|
| negative | add *not*: *She **isn't** tired.* | helper: *She **doesn't** work.* |
| question | flip: ***Is she** tired?* | helper: ***Does she** work?* |

Never mix the machines: ✗ *She doesn't be tired.* ✗ *Does she is a doctor?* ✗ *She isn't work.*

**ES:** Esta es la bifurcación de todo el idioma. Antes de negar o preguntar, pregúntate: **¿el verbo es *be*, o es un verbo normal?** Nunca mezcles las máquinas: ✗ *She doesn't be tired.* ✗ *Does she is a doctor?* ✗ *She isn't work.*

## Part 6 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| She don't like coffee. | She **doesn't** like coffee. | he/she/it → does |
| He no works here. | He **doesn't work** here. | "no + verb" is Spanish; English needs the helper |
| Does she works here? | Does she **work** here? | helper owns the -s; main verb = base |
| You want coffee? | **Do you want** coffee? | written questions need the helper, not just intonation |
| My father work in a factory. | My father work**s**... | third person -s |
| I don't can swim. | I **can't** swim. | modals never take do-support (FS-9) |
