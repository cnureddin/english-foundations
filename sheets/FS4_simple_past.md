# Foundation Sheet 4 · Simple Past
# Hoja de Fundamentos 4 · Pasado Simple

**Prerequisites / Prerrequisitos:** FS-0, FS-1, FS-2 (the do-machine — this sheet reuses it / esta hoja reutiliza la máquina *do*).

---

## Part 1 · What it does / Qué hace

**EN:** The simple past describes actions and states that are **finished**, at a known past time: *I visited my family last week.* It is the storytelling tense — with it you can narrate your day, your weekend, your life. Good news for a Spanish speaker: it covers the jobs of **both** the Spanish pretérito (*visité*) and, in most everyday sentences, the imperfecto (*visitaba*) — English usually doesn't force that choice at this level. Signal words: *yesterday, last night/week/year, ago (two days ago), in 2020, when I was a child.*

**ES:** El pasado simple describe acciones y estados **terminados**, en un momento pasado conocido: *I visited my family last week.* Es el tiempo de contar historias — con él puedes narrar tu día, tu fin de semana, tu vida. Buena noticia: cubre el trabajo del pretérito (*visité*) y, en la mayoría de las oraciones cotidianas, también del imperfecto (*visitaba*) — el inglés normalmente no te obliga a elegir a este nivel. Palabras señal: *yesterday, last night/week/year, ago, in 2020, when I was a child.*

## Part 2 · Regular verbs: + ed / Verbos regulares: + ed

**EN:** One beautiful simplification: in the past, the verb is the **same for every subject**. No -s, no changes by person. *I worked, she worked, they worked.*

**ES:** Una simplificación hermosa: en el pasado, el verbo es **igual para todos los sujetos**. Sin -s, sin cambios por persona. *I worked, she worked, they worked.*

Spelling / Ortografía: most verbs **+ed** (work → worked) · ends in -e **+d** (live → lived) · consonant + y → **-ied** (study → studied; but play → played) · short consonant-vowel-consonant doubles (stop → sto**pp**ed, plan → pla**nn**ed).

## Part 4 · *be* in the past: was / were / *Be* en el pasado: was / were

| Subject | Past form | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| I / he / she / it | **was** | I was tired. She was at home. | Estaba cansada. Estaba en casa. |
| you / we / they | **were** | They were happy. | Estaban felices. |

**EN:** *Be* keeps its FS-1 machine in the past: negative = + not (*wasn't, weren't*); questions = flip (*Was she at home? Were you tired?*). No *did* with *was/were* — ever.
**ES:** *Be* conserva su máquina de FS-1 en el pasado: negación = + not (*wasn't, weren't*); preguntas = inversión (*Was she at home?*). Nunca uses *did* con *was/were*.

### Mini-practice 1: 1. Yesterday I (go) ___ to the supermarket. 2. She (be) ___ very tired last night. 3. We (see) ___ a great movie. 4. He (study) ___ all weekend.

## Part 5 · Negatives and questions: the do-machine returns as *did* / Negaciones y preguntas: la máquina *do* vuelve como *did*

**EN:** Same machine as FS-2, one change: the helper is now **did** (for every subject). And the same law applies: the helper **carries the tense**, so the main verb snaps back to its **base form**. The past "jumps off" the main verb onto *did* — exactly like the -s did in FS-2.

* *She **went** home.* → the past lives in *went*.
* *She **didn't go** home.* → the past moved into *did*; the verb is base *go* again.
* ***Did** she **go** home?* → same.

**ES:** La misma máquina de FS-2, con un cambio: el auxiliar ahora es **did** (para todos los sujetos). Y aplica la misma ley: el auxiliar **lleva el tiempo**, así que el verbo principal vuelve a su **forma base**. El pasado "salta" del verbo principal a *did* — exactamente como la -s en FS-2.

| | Structure | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| negative | S + didn't + BASE | I **didn't watch** TV. | No vi la tele. |
| question | Did + S + BASE | **Did you sleep** well? | ¿Dormiste bien? |
| short answer | Yes/No + S + did(n't) | Yes, I **did**. / No, I **didn't**. | Sí. / No. |

⚠️ **The double-past crime / El crimen del doble pasado:** ✗ *I didn't **went**.* ✗ *Did she **bought** it?* → ✅ *I didn't **go**. Did she **buy** it?* One past per sentence, and *did* owns it.

## Part 6 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I didn't went to class. | I didn't **go** to class. | *did* carries the past; main verb = base |
| Did you saw the movie? | Did you **see** the movie? | same law |
| She no came to the party. | She **didn't come** to the party. | "no + verb" is Spanish; use the helper |
| Was you at home? | **Were** you at home? | you → were |
| Did she was sick? | **Was she** sick? | *be* never uses *did* — flip instead |
| I buyed a new phone. | I **bought** a new phone. | irregular verb — memory list |

## Part 7 · Practice / Práctica

**A. Put in the past / Pon en pasado:** 1. We (eat) ___ pizza last night. 2. He (not / call) ___ me yesterday. 3. (you / finish) ___ your homework? 4. They (be) ___ at the beach on Sunday. 5. She (write) ___ three emails this morning.

**B. Make negative AND question / Niega Y pregunta:** 1. You slept well. 2. Maria bought bread. 3. They were at school.

**C. Micro-story / Micro-historia:** write 3 sentences about yesterday using these skeletons: *Yesterday I + past verb + O* · *Then I + past verb + O + place* · *It was + description.* / escribe 3 oraciones sobre ayer con esos esqueletos.
