## Part 1 · The new tool: the clause / La herramienta nueva: la cláusula

**EN:** Until now, every sentence in this course was **one car**: one SUBJECT + one VERB (+ object or description). Here is the grammar word for that car: a **clause** = one subject–verb pair = **one engine**. Real English texts join two engines into one sentence — but only in certain legal ways. Your teacher will test three things: can you **name the type** of a sentence, can you **join** two clauses correctly, and can you **punctuate** the join. This sheet gives you all three.

**ES:** Hasta ahora, cada oración en este curso era **un coche**: un SUJETO + un VERBO (+ objeto o descripción). Esta es la palabra de gramática para ese coche: una **clause** (cláusula) = un par sujeto–verbo = **un motor**. Los textos reales en inglés unen dos motores en una sola oración — pero solo de ciertas maneras legales. Tu profesor evaluará tres cosas: si puedes **nombrar el tipo** de una oración, si puedes **unir** dos cláusulas correctamente, y si puedes **puntuar** la unión. Esta hoja te da las tres.

⭐ **The master skill / La habilidad maestra:** to analyze ANY sentence, first **count the engines** — count the subject–verb pairs. Everything else follows from that number. / Para analizar CUALQUIER oración, primero **cuenta los motores** — cuenta los pares sujeto–verbo. Todo lo demás sale de ese número.

## Part 2 · Simple sentences — one engine (even a wide one) / Oraciones simples — un motor (aunque sea ancho)

**EN:** A **simple sentence** has exactly one engine. But careful: one engine can be **wide**. The subject seat can hold a **team** (two drivers), and the verb seat can hold **two actions** for the same driver. That little *and* inside one seat does **not** create a second clause.

**ES:** Una **oración simple** tiene exactamente un motor. Pero cuidado: un motor puede ser **ancho**. El asiento del sujeto puede tener un **equipo** (dos conductores), y el asiento del verbo puede tener **dos acciones** para el mismo conductor. Ese pequeño *and* dentro de un asiento **no** crea una segunda cláusula.

| SUBJECT / SUJETO | VERB / VERBO | rest / resto | Type / Tipo | Español |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maria | cooks | dinner. | simple (1 engine) | María cocina la cena. |
| **Maria and Tom** | cook | dinner. | still simple — team subject / sujeto en equipo | María y Tom cocinan la cena. |
| Maria | **cooks and cleans** | every day. | still simple — two actions, one driver / dos acciones, un conductor | María cocina y limpia cada día. |

⚠️ **Agreement alert / Alerta de concordancia:** a team subject (*X and Y*) = *they* → the verb takes **no -s**: *Maria and Tom cook* (not ~~cooks~~). Full rules in FS-12. / Un sujeto en equipo (*X and Y*) = *they* → el verbo **sin -s**. Reglas completas en FS-12.

1. My sister and I watch movies on Fridays.
2. He works in a bank, and she teaches English.
3. The dog eats and sleeps all day.

## Part 3 · Compound sentences — two equal engines / Oraciones compuestas — dos motores iguales

**EN:** A **compound sentence** joins two complete clauses **as equals** — neither one depends on the other. English gives you exactly **two legal joiners**:

**Way 1 — comma + joiner word.** The seven joiner words are called **coordinating conjunctions**; the memory word is **FANBOYS**: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*. Pattern: `CLAUSE , fanboys CLAUSE`.

**ES:** Una **oración compuesta** une dos cláusulas completas **como iguales** — ninguna depende de la otra. El inglés te da exactamente **dos uniones legales**:

**Manera 1 — coma + palabra de unión.** Las siete palabras se llaman **coordinating conjunctions**; la palabra para memorizarlas es **FANBOYS**: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*. Patrón: `CLÁUSULA , fanboys CLÁUSULA`.

| Joiner | Meaning / Sentido | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| **and** | + (addition) | I cook, **and** he washes the dishes. | y |
| **but** | contrast | She studies a lot, **but** the exam is hard. | pero |
| **or** | choice / choice | We can walk, **or** we can take the bus. | o |
| **so** | → result | I was tired, **so** I went to bed. | así que |
| **for** | ← because (formal) | He stayed home, **for** he was sick. | porque (formal) |
| **yet** | contrast (≈ but) | It was cold, **yet** we went out. | sin embargo / pero |
| **nor** | and not (special: flips like a question) | He can't help you, **nor** can I. | ni |

**Way 2 — semicolon + connector + comma.** Connector words (your book calls them **conjunctive adverbs**): *however, therefore, also, then, instead, besides, moreover, otherwise, for example*. Pattern: `CLAUSE ; connector , CLAUSE`.

**Manera 2 — punto y coma + conector + coma.** Palabras conectoras (tu libro las llama **conjunctive adverbs**): *however, therefore, also, then, instead...* Patrón: `CLÁUSULA ; conector , CLÁUSULA`.

| | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|
| therefore (→ so) | Bob loved to surf**; therefore,** he lived near the beach. | por lo tanto |
| however (→ but) | I like coffee**; however,** I never drink it at night. | sin embargo |
| then | We ate dinner**; then,** we watched a movie. | luego |

**EN:** The connector can also move **inside** the second clause, wearing commas like earrings: *Joe, **however**, liked to hike.* Same meaning, same sentence type.
**ES:** El conector también puede moverse **adentro** de la segunda cláusula, con comas como aretes: *Joe, **however**, liked to hike.* Mismo sentido, mismo tipo de oración.

### Mini-practice 2 / Mini-práctica 2
Join each pair both ways (Way 1 and Way 2). / Une cada par de las dos maneras.
1. It was raining. We stayed home. *(so / therefore)*
2. She speaks English. She doesn't speak French. *(but / however)*

## Part 4 · Complex sentences — a boss engine + a helper engine / Oraciones complejas — un motor jefe + un motor ayudante

**EN:** A **complex sentence** also has two engines, but they are **not equals**: one clause is the **boss** (it can live alone), and the other is a **helper** that starts with a **subordinator**: *because, when, if, before, after, although, while*. The subordinator is like a leash — the moment you attach it, that clause **cannot stand alone anymore**. *I was tired* is a sentence. *Because I was tired* is not — it is hanging, waiting for a boss.

**The comma rule is about order:**
* Helper first → **comma** after it: ***Because I was tired,** I went to bed early.*
* Boss first → **no comma**: *I went to bed early **because I was tired**.*

**ES:** Una **oración compleja** también tiene dos motores, pero **no son iguales**: una cláusula es el **jefe** (puede vivir sola), y la otra es una **ayudante** que empieza con un **subordinador**: *because, when, if, before, after, although, while*. El subordinador es como una correa — en el momento que lo pones, esa cláusula **ya no puede estar sola**. *I was tired* es una oración. *Because I was tired* no lo es — queda colgando, esperando un jefe.

**La regla de la coma es sobre el orden:**
* Ayudante primero → **coma** después: ***Because I was tired,** I went to bed early.*
* Jefe primero → **sin coma**: *I went to bed early **because I was tired**.*

| Subordinator | Español | Example |
|---|---|---|
| because | porque | She stayed home **because** she was sick. |
| when | cuando | **When** I arrived, the class started. |
| if | si | **If** it rains, we will stay home. |
| before / after | antes de que / después de que | Call me **before** you leave. |
| although | aunque | **Although** it was cold, we went out. |
| while | mientras | He cooked **while** I studied. |

⭐ Good news / Buena noticia: this is the **easiest** type for you — Spanish builds complex sentences exactly the same way (*porque, cuando, si, aunque...*). Your instinct transfers directly. / Este es el tipo **más fácil** para ti — el español construye las oraciones complejas exactamente igual. Tu instinto se transfiere directo.

## Part 5 · The three crimes / Los tres crímenes

**EN:** Teachers grade these hard. All three crimes come from **counting engines wrong**.

**ES:** Los profesores califican esto con dureza. Los tres crímenes vienen de **contar mal los motores**.

| Crime / Crimen | What it is / Qué es | ❌ | ✅ Fix / Corrección |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fragment** / fragmento | a piece with no boss (often a helper clause alone) / un pedazo sin jefe | *Because he was late.* | *Because he was late, we started without him.* |
| **Comma splice** / empalme de coma | two engines glued with **only a comma** / dos motores pegados solo con coma | *It was late, we went home.* | *It was late**, so** we went home.* · *It was late**;** we went home.* |
| **Run-on** / oración corrida | two engines with **no glue at all** / dos motores sin pegamento | *It was late we went home.* | same fixes / mismas correcciones |

⚠️ **The Spanish trap / La trampa del español:** in Spanish, joining two clauses with just a comma is common and accepted (*"Era tarde, nos fuimos a casa"*). **In English it is always an error.** When you feel that comma coming, choose from the **fix menu**: (1) period — two sentences, (2) comma + FANBOYS, (3) semicolon (+ connector), (4) subordinator. / En español, unir dos cláusulas solo con coma es común y aceptado. **En inglés siempre es un error.** Cuando sientas venir esa coma, elige del **menú de correcciones**: (1) punto — dos oraciones, (2) coma + FANBOYS, (3) punto y coma (+ conector), (4) subordinador.

## Part 6 · The 3-step identification test / La prueba de identificación en 3 pasos

1. **Count the engines** — underline every subject–verb pair. / **Cuenta los motores** — subraya cada par sujeto–verbo.
2. **One engine** → **simple** (teams and double actions don't count as extra engines). / **Un motor** → **simple**.
3. **Two engines** → look at the glue / mira el pegamento:
   * FANBOYS or semicolon → **compound** / compuesta
   * subordinator (because, when, if...) → **complex** / compleja

## Part 7 · Practice / Práctica

**A. Name the type / Nombra el tipo** (simple, compound, complex):
1. My brother and his wife live in Madrid.
2. I wanted to call you, but my phone died.
3. When the movie ended, everyone clapped.
4. She made dinner; then, we played cards.
5. The students read and write every day.
6. If you study, you will pass.

**B. Join the two clauses three ways / Une las dos cláusulas de tres maneras** — (1) comma + FANBOYS, (2) semicolon + connector, (3) subordinator:
*I was hungry. I made a sandwich.*

**C. Crime scene: name the crime and fix it / Escena del crimen: nombra el crimen y corrígelo:**
1. Because the bus was late.
2. She loves music, she plays the guitar every day.
3. He opened the door the cat ran out.

## Answer key / Respuestas

**Mini-practice 1:** 1. one engine (team subject) — simple · 2. two engines — compound · 3. one engine (double action) — simple

**Mini-practice 2:** 1. *It was raining, so we stayed home.* / *It was raining; therefore, we stayed home.* · 2. *She speaks English, but she doesn't speak French.* / *She speaks English; however, she doesn't speak French.*

**A:** 1. simple (team subject) · 2. compound (, but) · 3. complex (When...) · 4. compound (; then,) · 5. simple (double action) · 6. complex (If...)

**B:** (1) *I was hungry, so I made a sandwich.* (2) *I was hungry; therefore, I made a sandwich.* (3) *Because I was hungry, I made a sandwich.*

**C:** 1. fragment → *Because the bus was late, I missed class.* · 2. comma splice → *She loves music, so she plays the guitar every day.* (or semicolon) · 3. run-on → *He opened the door, and the cat ran out.*

*Remember / Recuerda:* count the engines first. One engine = simple. Two equal engines need legal glue: comma + FANBOYS, or semicolon + connector. A subordinator (because, when, if...) makes a helper clause — helper first means comma. And a comma alone is never glue in English. / Cuenta los motores primero. Un motor = simple. Dos motores iguales necesitan pegamento legal: coma + FANBOYS, o punto y coma + conector. Un subordinador (because, when, if...) crea una cláusula ayudante — si va primero, lleva coma. Y una coma sola nunca es pegamento en inglés.
