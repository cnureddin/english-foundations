## Part 1 · General and specific: the shape of a paragraph / General y específico: la forma de un párrafo

**EN:** Writers and readers communicate through **levels**. A **general** sentence covers many things; a **specific** sentence covers one thing. Look at this ladder — each step down is more specific:

> food → fruit → apples → the green apples in my garden

A **paragraph** is one idea built from these levels: it starts with its most **general** sentence — the **topic sentence** — and then supports it with **specific** sentences (reasons, examples, steps, details). Often it ends with a short closing sentence. When you read, the test question is: **"Which sentence covers all the others?"** That one is the topic sentence. When you write, the same shape in reverse: say the big idea first, then prove it with small, specific sentences.

**ES:** Los escritores y lectores se comunican por **niveles**. Una oración **general** cubre muchas cosas; una oración **específica** cubre una sola. Mira esta escalera — cada escalón hacia abajo es más específico:

> comida → fruta → manzanas → las manzanas verdes de mi jardín

Un **párrafo** es una idea construida con estos niveles: empieza con su oración más **general** — la **topic sentence** (oración principal) — y luego la apoya con oraciones **específicas** (razones, ejemplos, pasos, detalles). A menudo termina con una oración corta de cierre. Al leer, la pregunta de prueba es: **"¿Cuál oración cubre a todas las demás?"** Esa es la topic sentence. Al escribir, la misma forma al revés: di la idea grande primero, y luego pruébala con oraciones pequeñas y específicas.

| Layer / Capa | Job / Trabajo | Example / Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **Topic sentence** | the general idea / la idea general | *My city is a good place for families.* |
| Support 1, 2, 3... | specific reasons, examples, steps / razones, ejemplos, pasos específicos | *It has many parks. The schools are good. People are friendly.* |
| Closing / Cierre | repeat the idea in new words / repite la idea con otras palabras | *For these reasons, families love living here.* |

## Part 2 · Connector families: the traffic signs of a paragraph / Familias de conectores: las señales de tráfico del párrafo

**EN:** Connectors tell the reader **what relationship** comes next: another step? a reason? an opposite? Readers use them to follow you; teachers use them to grade you. Learn them as **families by job**. (Punctuation for *however / therefore* is in FS-11 Part 3.)

**ES:** Los conectores le dicen al lector **qué relación** viene: ¿otro paso? ¿una razón? ¿un contraste? Los lectores los usan para seguirte; los profesores los usan para calificarte. Apréndelos como **familias por trabajo**. (La puntuación de *however / therefore* está en FS-11, Parte 3.)

| Job / Trabajo | Connectors / Conectores | Español |
|---|---|---|
| **Sequence** (order in time) / secuencia | First, ... Then, ... Next, ... After that, ... Finally, ... | Primero, Luego, Después, Finalmente |
| **Cause → effect** / causa → efecto | because, so, therefore, as a result | porque, así que, por lo tanto, como resultado |
| **Contrast** / contraste | but, however, although | pero, sin embargo, aunque |
| **Addition** / adición | and, also, in addition | y, también, además |
| **Example** / ejemplo | for example, such as | por ejemplo, como |
| **Opinion** / opinión | I think (that)..., In my opinion, ... | Creo que..., En mi opinión, ... |

⭐ One connector per sentence is enough. Two supports with *also* and one *for example* already make a paragraph sound organized. / Un conector por oración es suficiente. Dos apoyos con *also* y un *for example* ya hacen que un párrafo suene organizado.

## Part 3 · The writing rule, always / La regla de escritura, siempre

**EN:** Ideas in Spanish, sentences in English. Brainstorm your content as short Spanish fragments (words, not sentences). Then build **each English sentence from a skeleton below**, one at a time. Never translate a full Spanish sentence. Self-check before submitting: **"If you can't label its seats, don't submit it."**

**ES:** Ideas en español, oraciones en inglés. Haz tu lluvia de ideas con fragmentos cortos en español (palabras, no oraciones). Luego construye **cada oración en inglés con un esqueleto de abajo**, una por una. Nunca traduzcas una oración completa del español. Auto-revisión antes de entregar: **"Si no puedes etiquetar sus asientos, no la entregues."**

## Part 4 · The four paragraph skeletons / Los cuatro esqueletos de párrafo

### 4a · Story paragraph (narrative — past tense, FS-4) / Párrafo de historia (narrativo — pasado)

**EN:** A story paragraph tells about **one event in the past**, in time order. The engine of every sentence is a **past verb**.
**ES:** Un párrafo de historia cuenta **un evento del pasado**, en orden de tiempo. El motor de cada oración es un **verbo en pasado**.

```
Last (year / summer / week), I + past verb + ...     ← topic sentence
First, I + past verb + OBJECT.
Then, I + past verb + OBJECT + place.
Finally, I + past verb + ...
It was + ADJECTIVE, and I + past verb + ...          ← closing feeling
```

Model fragment / Fragmento modelo: *Last summer, I visited my family in Colombia. First, I stayed with my grandmother for one week. It was wonderful, and I felt very happy.*

### 4b · Process paragraph (how to do something) / Párrafo de proceso (cómo hacer algo)

**EN:** A process = steps in a fixed order. The trick from your course: make the topic **small** enough for one paragraph. Use sequence connectors + simple present, and you may speak to the reader with *you*.
**ES:** Un proceso = pasos en un orden fijo. El truco de tu curso: haz el tema **suficientemente pequeño** para un párrafo. Usa conectores de secuencia + presente simple, y puedes hablarle al lector con *you*.

```
It is easy to + verb + ... if you follow these steps.   ← topic sentence
First, you + base verb + OBJECT.
Then, you + base verb + OBJECT.
Next, you + base verb + ...
Finally, you + base verb + ... , and the ... is ready.
```

Model fragment / Fragmento modelo: *It is easy to make Colombian coffee if you follow these steps. First, you boil the water. Then, you add two spoons of coffee.*

### 4c · Cause-and-effect paragraph (reasons) / Párrafo de causa y efecto (razones)

**EN:** This paragraph answers **why**. The topic sentence names the situation; each support gives one reason, from small to biggest.
**ES:** Este párrafo responde **por qué**. La topic sentence nombra la situación; cada apoyo da una razón, de menor a mayor.

```
I + verb + ... for several reasons.                  ← topic sentence
One reason is that + CLAUSE.
Also, + CLAUSE.
Most of all, + CLAUSE + because + CLAUSE.
As a result, + CLAUSE.                               ← closing effect
```

Model fragment / Fragmento modelo: *I stopped drinking soda for several reasons. One reason is that it is expensive. Most of all, I stopped because it was bad for my health.*

### 4d · Opinion paragraph / Párrafo de opinión

**EN:** State your position in the topic sentence, give 2 reasons, add one example, close by repeating the position in new words.
**ES:** Da tu posición en la topic sentence, ofrece 2 razones, añade un ejemplo, y cierra repitiendo la posición con otras palabras.

```
In my opinion, + SUBJECT + is / should + ...         ← position
One reason is that + CLAUSE.
Also, + CLAUSE.
For example, + CLAUSE.
For these reasons, I think (that) + CLAUSE.          ← closing
```

Model fragment / Fragmento modelo: *In my opinion, students should sleep eight hours. One reason is that sleep helps memory. For these reasons, I think sleep is more important than extra study.*

## Part 5 · Fact or opinion? / ¿Hecho u opinión?

**EN:** Your course tests this as a reading skill. A **fact** can be **checked and proven** — with numbers, records, laws, measurements: *"Heart disease is the leading cause of death in America."* You can verify it. An **opinion** is a belief or judgment — reasonable people can disagree: *"Rock lyrics are more poetic than rap lyrics."* **Signal words of opinion:** *best, worst, better than, should, beautiful, fun, wrong, interesting.* The test question: **"Could I check this in a book or database?"** Yes → fact. No, it depends on taste or values → opinion. Careful: an opinion stays an opinion even if you agree with it, and a fact stays a fact even if it is false — *"There are more cell phones in Japan than people"* is a fact-type statement because it can be **checked**.

**ES:** Tu curso evalúa esto como habilidad de lectura. Un **hecho** se puede **verificar y probar** — con números, registros, leyes, mediciones. Una **opinión** es una creencia o juicio — personas razonables pueden estar en desacuerdo. **Palabras señal de opinión:** *best, worst, better than, should, beautiful, fun, wrong, interesting.* La pregunta de prueba: **"¿Podría verificar esto en un libro o una base de datos?"** Sí → hecho. No, depende del gusto o los valores → opinión. Cuidado: una opinión sigue siendo opinión aunque estés de acuerdo, y una afirmación tipo hecho sigue siendo tipo hecho aunque resulte falsa — lo importante es que **se puede verificar**.

### Mini-practice 2 / Mini-práctica 2 — Fact (F) or Opinion (O)?
1. The chicken sandwich costs more than the cheeseburger.
2. Curly hair looks better than straight hair.
3. Skateboarding on public property is against the law here.
4. Copying homework is wrong.

## Part 6 · Email frames: formal and informal / Marcos de email: formal e informal

**EN:** An email is a tiny paragraph with a **frame** around it. The frame changes with the reader: a teacher or company → **formal**; a friend → **informal**. Choose ONE column and stay in it — mixing frames is the classic error.

**ES:** Un email es un párrafo pequeño con un **marco** alrededor. El marco cambia según el lector: un profesor o una empresa → **formal**; un amigo → **informal**. Elige UNA columna y quédate en ella — mezclar marcos es el error clásico.

| Part / Parte | Formal | Informal |
|---|---|---|
| Greeting / Saludo | Dear Mr. Lopez, / Dear Sir or Madam, | Hi Ana, / Hello! |
| Opening / Apertura | I am writing to + base verb ... | Thanks for your message! / How are you? |
| Body / Cuerpo | 2–4 short sentences, built from skeletons / 2–4 oraciones cortas, con esqueletos | same / igual |
| Pre-closing / Pre-cierre | Thank you for your time. | Write soon! |
| Sign-off / Despedida | Best regards, + full name | See you soon, / Love, + first name |

Email skeleton / Esqueleto de email:
```
GREETING,
I am writing to + base verb + ... (formal)  |  Thanks for ... (informal)
SENTENCE 1 (a detail / un detalle).
SENTENCE 2 (a question or suggestion: Could you...? / You should + base verb...).
PRE-CLOSING.
SIGN-OFF,
NAME
```
