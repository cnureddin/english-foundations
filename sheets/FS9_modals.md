# Hoja de Fundamentos 9 · Modales: can, could, should, must, have to

**Prerequisites / Prerrequisitos:** FS-0 (helpers), FS-2 (do-machine — to know when NOT to use it / para saber cuándo NO usarla). You already met one modal: *will* (FS-5).

---

## Part 1 · What modals do / Qué hacen los modales

**EN:** A modal is a special helper verb that adds a **flavor of attitude** to the main verb: ability, permission, advice, obligation, possibility. The modal carries the attitude; the main verb carries the action. *I **can** swim* = ability + swim. Meet the family:

| Modal | Flavor / Sabor | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| **can** | ability, permission, informal requests | I can drive. Can I open the window? | puedo / sé (hacer algo) |
| **could** | past ability; polite requests | I could swim at age 5. Could you help me? | podía / ¿podrías? |
| **should** | advice / consejo | You should sleep more. | deberías |
| **must** | strong obligation, or logical certainty | You must wear a seatbelt. She must be tired. | debes / debe de estar |
| **have to** | obligation from outside (rules, bosses) | I have to work on Saturday. | tengo que |

**ES:** Un modal es un auxiliar especial que añade un **sabor de actitud** al verbo principal: habilidad, permiso, consejo, obligación, posibilidad. El modal lleva la actitud; el verbo principal lleva la acción. *I **can** swim* = habilidad + nadar. ⚠️ Nota: para "sé nadar / sé cocinar" (habilidad aprendida), el inglés usa **can**, no *know*: ✗ ~~I know swim~~ → ✅ *I can swim*.

## Part 2 · The three laws of modals / Las tres leyes de los modales

**EN:** Modals are wonderfully lazy — after the do-machine of FS-2/FS-4, these rules are a vacation. Every true modal obeys three laws:

1. **Modal + BASE verb, never "to".** *I can **swim**.* ✗ ~~I can to swim~~ ✗ ~~I can swimming~~
2. **No -s, ever — on the modal or the verb.** *She can drive.* ✗ ~~She cans drive~~ ✗ ~~She can drives~~
3. **No do-machine.** Modals make their own negatives and questions, like *be*:
   * negative = modal + not: *can't / cannot, couldn't, shouldn't, mustn't*
   * question = flip the modal: ***Can you** drive? **Should I** call her?*
   * short answers: *Yes, I can. / No, you shouldn't.*
   * ✗ ~~Do you can drive?~~ ✗ ~~She doesn't can come.~~ → ✅ *Can you drive? She can't come.*

**ES:** Los modales son maravillosamente perezosos — después de la máquina *do* de FS-2/FS-4, estas reglas son unas vacaciones. Todo modal verdadero obedece tres leyes: (1) **modal + verbo BASE, nunca "to"**; (2) **sin -s, jamás** — ni en el modal ni en el verbo; (3) **sin máquina *do***: negación = modal + not (*can't, shouldn't*), pregunta = invertir el modal (*Can you drive?*).

### Mini-practice 1: 1. She ___ (can / speak) three languages. 2. ___ (should / I / call) a doctor? 3. He ___ (can / not / come) tonight.

## Part 3 · The exception: *have to* / La excepción: *have to*

**EN:** *Have to* means obligation like *must*, but grammatically it is **not** a true modal — it behaves like a normal verb, so it **does** use the do-machine and **does** take -s:

| | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | She **has to** work today. | Tiene que trabajar hoy. |
| negative | She **doesn't have to** work. (= no obligation!) | No tiene que trabajar (no es necesario). |
| question | **Does she have to** work? | ¿Tiene que trabajar? |
| past | I **had to** leave early. | Tuve que salir temprano. |

⚠️ Meaning alert: ***mustn't*** = prohibited (*You mustn't smoke here* — prohibido), but ***don't have to*** = not necessary (*You don't have to come* — no es obligatorio, pero puedes). Spanish "no tienes que" maps to *don't have to*, NOT to *mustn't*.

**ES:** *Have to* significa obligación como *must*, pero gramaticalmente **no** es un modal verdadero — se comporta como verbo normal: **sí** usa la máquina *do* y **sí** lleva -s (*she has to*). ⚠️ Alerta de significado: ***mustn't*** = prohibido, pero ***don't have to*** = no es necesario. El "no tienes que" español corresponde a *don't have to*, NO a *mustn't*.

## Part 4 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I can to drive. | I can **drive**. | law 1: base verb, no "to" |
| She cans cook. / She can cooks. | She **can cook**. | law 2: no -s anywhere |
| Do you can help me? | **Can you** help me? | law 3: flip the modal |
| I don't can swim. | I **can't** swim. | law 3 |
| He must works. | He must **work**. | base verb |
| She have to study. | She **has to** study. | *have to* is a normal verb → -s |
| You mustn't come if you're busy. (meaning: not necessary) | You **don't have to** come... | mustn't = prohibited |
| I know swim. | I **can** swim. | learned ability = can |

## Part 5 · Practice / Práctica

**A. Choose the modal / Elige el modal (can, could, should, must, have to):** 1. You look tired. You ___ rest. 2. In this office, everyone ___ wear an ID card. (rule) 3. ___ you pass me the salt, please? (polite) 4. When I was a child, I ___ climb trees. 5. I ___ finish this report today — my boss needs it.

**B. Negatives & questions / Negaciones y preguntas:** 1. She can dance. (question) 2. We should buy it. (negative) 3. He has to get up early. (question) 4. You must park here. (negative — prohibited!)

**C. Fix / Corrige:** 1. Does she can drive? 2. I must to go now. 3. He cans speak French. 4. You no should eat that.
