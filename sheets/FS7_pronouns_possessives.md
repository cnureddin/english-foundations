## Part 1 · Why pronouns have two shapes / Por qué los pronombres tienen dos formas

**EN:** A pronoun is a short word that replaces a noun so you don't repeat it. Here is the key insight: in English, the pronoun's shape depends on **which seat it sits in**. One shape for the SUBJECT seat, another for the OBJECT seat (and after prepositions). Same person, two costumes:

* ***She** loves Marco.* (subject seat → *she*)
* *Marco loves **her**.* (object seat → *her*)

Spanish works differently — it has *yo/me/mí, él/lo/le...* with placement rules that don't transfer. Forget the Spanish system while writing English; just ask: **which seat?**

**ES:** Un pronombre es una palabra corta que reemplaza a un sustantivo para no repetirlo. La idea clave: en inglés, la forma del pronombre depende de **en qué asiento se sienta**. Una forma para el asiento del SUJETO, otra para el asiento del OBJETO (y después de preposiciones). La misma persona, dos disfraces: ***She** loves Marco* (sujeto) / *Marco loves **her*** (objeto). El español funciona distinto (*yo/me/mí, él/lo/le...*) con reglas de posición que no se transfieren. Olvida el sistema español al escribir inglés; solo pregunta: **¿qué asiento?**

## Part 2 · The full table / La tabla completa

| SUBJECT seat / SUJETO | OBJECT seat & after prepositions / OBJETO y tras preposición | Possessive + noun / Posesivo + sustantivo | Possessive alone / Posesivo solo | Español |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | me | my | mine | yo / me / mi / mío |
| you | you | your | yours | tú / te / tu / tuyo |
| he | him | his | his | él / lo-le / su / suyo |
| she | her | her | hers | ella / la-le / su / suyo |
| it | it | its | — | (cosa/animal) |
| we | us | our | ours | nosotros / nos / nuestro |
| they | them | their | theirs | ellos / los-les / su / suyo |

**Seat check / Prueba de asiento:**

| SUBJECT | VERB | OBJECT | ✔ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **He** | called | **me**. | Él me llamó. |
| **We** | invited | **them**. | Los invitamos. |
| **She** | works | with **him**. | after preposition → object shape |

⚠️ Common trap: after prepositions, always the object shape — *with **me***, *for **her***, *between you and **me*** (✗ ~~between you and I~~).

## Part 3 · Possessives: two tools / Posesivos: dos herramientas

**EN:** Tool 1 — **possessive + noun** (*my, your, his...*): sits before a noun like an adjective. *This is **my** phone.* Tool 2 — **possessive alone** (*mine, yours, his...*): replaces the whole "my + noun" package. *This phone is **mine**.* Never combine: ✗ ~~This is mine phone~~.

**ES:** Herramienta 1 — **posesivo + sustantivo** (*my, your, his...*): va antes de un sustantivo, como adjetivo. *This is **my** phone.* Herramienta 2 — **posesivo solo** (*mine, yours...*): reemplaza el paquete completo "mi + sustantivo". *This phone is **mine**.* Nunca los combines: ✗ ~~This is mine phone~~.

⚠️ **The *su* problem / El problema de *su*:** Spanish *su* can mean his, her, its, their, or your. English forces you to choose: *her book* (de ella), *his book* (de él), *their book* (de ellos). Before writing "su," decide **whose exactly**.

⚠️ **his vs. her follows the OWNER / *his* y *her* siguen al DUEÑO:** *Maria loves **her** dog* (owner is female) — even if the dog is male. *Marco loves **his** sister.*

## Part 4 · 's — possession the English way / 's — la posesión al estilo inglés

**EN:** Spanish says "the book **of** Maria" (*el libro de María*). English flips it and uses **'s** on the owner: ***Maria's** book*. Owner first, then the thing. *My **sister's** car. The **teacher's** desk.* For plural owners already ending in -s, just the apostrophe: *my **parents'** house.* "The book of Maria" is understandable but sounds foreign — train the 's.

**ES:** El español dice "el libro **de** María". El inglés lo invierte y pone **'s** en el dueño: ***Maria's** book*. Primero el dueño, luego la cosa. *My **sister's** car.* Para dueños en plural que ya terminan en -s, solo el apóstrofo: *my **parents'** house*. "The book of Maria" se entiende pero suena extranjero — entrena el 's.

⚠️ **its vs. it's** — the classic: **its** = possessive (*The dog ate **its** food*), **it's** = it is (*(**It's** cold today*). The apostrophe means "is," not possession, in this one case.

## Part 5 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| Me and my friend went out. | **My friend and I** went out. | subject seat → I (and go last / y va al final) |
| He called I. | He called **me**. | object seat → me |
| This is the car of my brother. | This is my **brother's** car. | 's, owner first |
| Maria loves his dog. (Maria's own dog) | Maria loves **her** dog. | possessive follows the owner |
| This is mine book. | This is **my** book. / This book is **mine**. | one tool at a time |
| The company changed it's logo. | ...changed **its** logo. | its = possessive; it's = it is |
| Is the house of they. | It's **their** house. / The house is **theirs**. | choose the right tool |

**C. Rewrite with 's / Reescribe con 's:** 1. the car of my father → ___ 2. the toys of the children → ___ 3. the house of my parents → ___

**C:** 1. my father's car · 2. the children's toys · 3. my parents' house

---

*Remember / Recuerda:* the seat chooses the pronoun — subject seat: *I, he, she, we, they*; object seat and after prepositions: *me, him, her, us, them*. Possession goes owner-first with **'s**, and *su* must become a choice: his? her? their? your? / El asiento elige el pronombre — sujeto: *I, he, she, we, they*; objeto y tras preposición: *me, him, her, us, them*. La posesión va con el dueño primero + **'s**, y *su* debe convertirse en una decisión: ¿his? ¿her? ¿their? ¿your?
