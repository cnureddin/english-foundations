# Foundation Note 9a · Polite Machines: Requests, Offers, and Invitations
# Nota de Fundamentos 9a · Máquinas de Cortesía: Peticiones, Ofertas e Invitaciones

**Prerequisites / Prerrequisitos:** FS-9 (modals). This note extends FS-9 Part 1: same grammar, new **social jobs**. / Esta nota extiende FS-9, Parte 1: misma gramática, nuevos **trabajos sociales**.

---

## Part 1 · One law, four jobs / Una ley, cuatro trabajos

**EN:** All the polite machines in this note obey the FS-9 law: **modal + BASE verb** (no *to*, no *-s*, no do-machine — the modal flips to make a question). What changes is the **job**: are you asking someone to act? asking for a thing? asking permission? or offering something? English has a fixed, formula-like pattern for each job. Learn them as **whole frames**, not word by word — they are the sentences you will say most often in real life.

**ES:** Todas las máquinas de cortesía de esta nota obedecen la ley de FS-9: **modal + verbo BASE** (sin *to*, sin *-s*, sin máquina *do* — el modal se invierte para preguntar). Lo que cambia es el **trabajo**: ¿pides que alguien actúe? ¿pides una cosa? ¿pides permiso? ¿u ofreces algo? El inglés tiene un patrón fijo, como fórmula, para cada trabajo. Apréndelos como **marcos completos**, no palabra por palabra — son las oraciones que más dirás en la vida real.

| Job / Trabajo | Frame / Marco | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Ask someone to DO something / Pedir que alguien HAGA algo | **Can / Could you + base verb...?** | **Could you** open the door, please? | ¿Podrías abrir la puerta? |
| 2. Ask FOR a thing / Pedir una COSA | **Can / Could I have + noun...?** | **Could I have** the menu, please? | ¿Me da el menú, por favor? |
| 3. Ask PERMISSION to do something / Pedir PERMISO | **Can / Could I + base verb...?** | **Could I** use your phone charger? | ¿Podría usar tu cargador? |
| 4. OFFER or INVITE / OFRECER o INVITAR | **Would you like + noun / to + verb?** | **Would you like** some coffee? **Would you like to** eat with us? | ¿Quieres un café? ¿Te gustaría comer con nosotros? |

⭐ **Could is the polite volume knob / Could es la perilla de cortesía:** *can* = normal, *could* = more polite. Same meaning, softer sound. With strangers, teachers, and shops: use **could** + **please**. / *can* = normal, *could* = más cortés. Con desconocidos, profesores y tiendas: usa **could** + **please**.

## Part 2 · The two traps / Las dos trampas

**⚠️ Trap 1 — *Would you like* ≠ *Do you like*.**
**EN:** *Do you like coffee?* asks about your taste **in general** (FS-2). *Would you like coffee?* is an **offer, right now**. If a host asks *"Would you like some cake?"* and you answer *"Yes, I like cake"*, you answered the wrong question — say ***Yes, please*** or ***No, thanks***. Spanish makes exactly the same difference, so borrow your instinct: *¿Te gusta...?* = *Do you like...?* · *¿Te gustaría...?* = *Would you like...?*

**ES:** *Do you like coffee?* pregunta por tu gusto **en general** (FS-2). *Would you like coffee?* es una **oferta, ahora mismo**. El español hace exactamente la misma diferencia — usa tu instinto: *¿Te gusta...?* = *Do you like...?* · *¿Te gustaría...?* = *Would you like...?* Respuestas a una oferta: ***Yes, please*** / ***No, thanks***.

**⚠️ Trap 2 — *Do you mind if I...?* flips the answer / voltea la respuesta.**
**EN:** *Do you mind if I sit here?* literally asks *"is it a problem for you?"* So the permission answer is ***No*** (*No, go ahead* = no problem = yes, you can). If you want to say "you can't," you say *Sorry, ...* — almost never a hard *yes, I mind*.
**ES:** *Do you mind if I sit here?* pregunta literalmente *"¿te molesta si...?"* Así que la respuesta que da permiso es ***No*** (*No, go ahead* = no me molesta = sí puedes). Igual que en español: *"¿Te molesta si me siento?" — "No, para nada."*

## Part 3 · Saying what you want, politely / Decir lo que quieres, con cortesía

**EN:** In a shop, cafe, or office, *I want* sounds hard. The soft frame is **I'd like** (= *I would like*) + noun or + *to* + base verb:
**ES:** En una tienda, café u oficina, *I want* suena duro. El marco suave es **I'd like** (= *I would like*) + sustantivo o + *to* + verbo base:

* *I'd like a coffee, please.* / *Quisiera un café, por favor.*
* *I'd like to make an appointment.* / *Quisiera hacer una cita.*

## Part 4 · Mini-practice / Mini-práctica

Choose the frame and complete. / Elige el marco y completa.
1. You're cold; the window is open. Ask the man next to you: ____________ (close the window)
2. In a cafe, you want the bill: ____________ (the bill)
3. You have two concert tickets. Invite your friend: ____________ (come to a concert)
4. You answer: "____________?" — "No, thanks. I'm not hungry."
5. You want to try on some shoes in a shop: ____________ (try them on)

---

## Answer key / Respuestas

1. **Could you close** the window, please? · 2. **Could I have** the bill, please? · 3. **Would you like to come** to a concert (tomorrow)? · 4. **Would you like** something to eat? · 5. **Could I try** them on, please? (or: *I'd like to try them on, please.*)

*Remember / Recuerda:* one law — modal + base verb. Four jobs — Could you (do it), Could I have (a thing), Could I (do it myself), Would you like (an offer). *Could* + *please* is your polite volume knob, and answer offers with *Yes, please / No, thanks*. / Una ley — modal + verbo base. Cuatro trabajos — Could you (que lo hagas), Could I have (una cosa), Could I (hacerlo yo), Would you like (una oferta). *Could* + *please* es tu perilla de cortesía, y a las ofertas se responde *Yes, please / No, thanks*.
