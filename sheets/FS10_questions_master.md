## Part 1 · One formula for every question / Una fórmula para toda pregunta

**EN:** You have learned questions tense by tense. Here is the secret: **it was always the same machine.** Every English question follows one word order, remembered as **QUASM**:

> **QU**estion word (optional) + **A**uxiliary (helper) + **S**ubject + **M**ain verb + rest
> *Where* + *does* + *she* + *work* + —?

The only decision is **which helper goes in the A slot** — and you already know the answer from each sheet: it is whichever helper that tense uses.

**ES:** Has aprendido las preguntas tiempo por tiempo. Este es el secreto: **siempre fue la misma máquina.** Toda pregunta en inglés sigue un solo orden de palabras, recordado como **QUASM**:

> Palabra inte**RR**ogativa (opcional) + **A**uxiliar + **S**ujeto + Verbo principal (**M**ain) + resto

La única decisión es **qué auxiliar va en la casilla A** — y ya la sabes de cada hoja: es el auxiliar que usa ese tiempo.

## Part 2 · Choosing the A slot / Elegir la casilla A

| Your sentence uses... | Helper for the question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| *be* alone (FS-1) | flip **am/is/are/was/were** | **Is** she a doctor? **Were** you tired? |
| simple present (FS-2) | **do / does** (verb → base) | **Does** she work here? |
| simple past (FS-4) | **did** (verb → base) | **Did** you sleep well? |
| continuous *be + -ing* (FS-3) | flip the **be** | **Are** you listening? **Was** he cooking? |
| *going to* (FS-5) | flip the **be** | **Is** she going to call? |
| *will* (FS-5) | flip **will** | **Will** they come? |
| modal (FS-9) | flip the **modal** | **Can** you drive? **Should** I wait? |

**EN:** Notice the deep pattern: if the sentence **already has a helper** (*be*, *will*, a modal), you just flip it. Only when there is **no helper** (simple present/past) do you hire *do/does/did* — and it steals the -s or the past, leaving the main verb in base form.
**ES:** Nota el patrón profundo: si la oración **ya tiene un auxiliar** (*be*, *will*, un modal), solo lo inviertes. Solo cuando **no hay auxiliar** (presente/pasado simple) contratas a *do/does/did* — que se roba la -s o el pasado, dejando el verbo principal en forma base.

## Part 3 · The QU slot: question words / La casilla QU: palabras interrogativas

| Word | Asks about / Pregunta por | Example | Español |
|---|---|---|---|
| **what** | thing / cosa | What do you want? | ¿qué? |
| **where** | place / lugar | Where does she live? | ¿dónde? |
| **when** | time / tiempo | When did they arrive? | ¿cuándo? |
| **who** | person / persona | Who are you calling? | ¿quién? |
| **why** | reason / razón | Why is he crying? | ¿por qué? |
| **how** | manner / manera | How do you say...? | ¿cómo? |
| **how much / how many** | quantity (noncount / count, FS-6) | How much money? How many friends? | ¿cuánto/s? |
| **which** | choice among options | Which color do you prefer? | ¿cuál? |
| **whose** | owner (FS-7) | Whose phone is this? | ¿de quién? |

Yes/No questions simply leave the QU slot empty: *(—) Does she work here?*

### Mini-practice 1 — build with QUASM: 1. (where / you / live)? 2. (what / she / do) yesterday? 3. (why / they / be) late? 4. (how many / languages / he / can / speak)?

## Part 4 · The exception: subject questions / La excepción: preguntas de sujeto

**EN:** One special case. When the question word **is itself the subject** — you're asking *who did it*, not *what someone did* — there is nothing to flip and no helper is hired. The question word simply sits in the subject seat and the sentence keeps normal statement order:

* ***Who called** you?* (who = subject; ✗ ~~Who did call you?~~) — answer: *Maria called me.*
* ***What happened**?* (✗ ~~What did happen?~~)
* Compare: *Who **did you call**?* — here *you* is the subject and *who* is the object, so QUASM applies.

Test: if the answer replaces the question word in the SUBJECT seat (*Maria called*), it's a subject question — no helper.

**ES:** Un caso especial. Cuando la palabra interrogativa **es el sujeto** — preguntas *quién lo hizo*, no *qué hizo alguien* — no hay nada que invertir y no se contrata auxiliar. La palabra interrogativa simplemente se sienta en el asiento del sujeto y la oración mantiene el orden normal: ***Who called** you?* (✗ ~~Who did call you?~~). Compara: *Who **did you call**?* — ahí *you* es el sujeto y *who* es el objeto, así que aplica QUASM. Prueba: si la respuesta reemplaza a la palabra interrogativa en el asiento del SUJETO (*Maria called*), es pregunta de sujeto — sin auxiliar.

## Part 5 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| Where she lives? | Where **does she live**? | no helper hired; Spanish-style order |
| What you are doing? | What **are you** doing? | flip the be (A before S) |
| Why he didn't come? | Why **didn't he** come? | helper before subject, always |
| Who did call you? | Who **called** you? | subject question — no helper |
| How much books do you have? | How **many** books...? | count noun → many (FS-6) |
| You can help me? (written) | **Can you** help me? | flip the modal |
| What means "toalla"? | What **does** "toalla" **mean**? | classic classroom question — QUASM applies! |
