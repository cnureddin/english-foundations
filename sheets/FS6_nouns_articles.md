## Part 1 · Plurals / Plurales

**EN:** Most nouns add **-s** for plural, with the same spelling family you know from FS-2's verb -s:
**ES:** La mayoría de los sustantivos añaden **-s** para el plural, con la misma familia ortográfica de la -s verbal de FS-2:

| Rule / Regla | Examples |
|---|---|
| most nouns +s | book → books, apple → apples |
| -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, -o → +es | watch → watches, bus → buses, tomato → tomatoes |
| consonant + y → -ies | city → cities, baby → babies (but day → days) |
| -f / -fe → -ves | knife → knives, leaf → leaves |
| **irregular — memorize** | man → **men**, woman → **women**, child → **children**, person → **people**, foot → **feet**, tooth → **teeth** |

⚠️ *People* is already plural: *People **are** friendly here.* ✗ ~~peoples~~, ✗ ~~people is~~.

## Part 2 · The big split: count vs. noncount / La gran división: contables vs. incontables

**EN:** English divides all nouns into two families, and the family decides everything downstream (plural or not, *a* or no *a*, *many* or *much*):

* **Count nouns** — things you can count one by one: *one apple, two apples.* They have plurals and can take *a/an*.
* **Noncount nouns** — substances, materials, and abstract "masses" you can't count directly: *water, money, bread, music, information, advice, furniture,* and — importantly for you — ***homework***. They have **no plural** and **never take *a/an***. To count them, you count a container or unit: *a glass of water, a piece of advice, three assignments (not three homeworks).* 

The trap: several nouns that are countable in Spanish are noncount in English. Burn these in:

| Español (contable) | English (noncount) | ✅ How to say it |
|---|---|---|
| las tareas | homework | I have a lot of homework. / two assignments |
| las informaciones | information | some information / a piece of information |
| los consejos | advice | some advice / a piece of advice |
| los muebles | furniture | some furniture / a piece of furniture |
| las noticias | news | some news / a piece of news (news + is!) |
| el dinero (ok) / pesos | money | much money / a lot of money |

**ES:** El inglés divide todos los sustantivos en dos familias, y la familia decide todo lo demás (plural o no, *a* o sin *a*, *many* o *much*): **contables** (se cuentan uno a uno: *one apple, two apples*; tienen plural y aceptan *a/an*) e **incontables** (sustancias y "masas": *water, money, bread, information, advice* y — importante para ti — ***homework***; **no tienen plural** y **nunca llevan *a/an***; para contarlos, cuentas un recipiente o unidad: *a glass of water, a piece of advice*). La trampa: varios sustantivos contables en español son incontables en inglés — memoriza la tabla.

**Quantity words / Palabras de cantidad:** count → *many, a few* (*many books, a few friends*) · noncount → *much, a little* (*much time, a little water*) · both → *some, a lot of, any*.

## Part 3 · Articles: a / an / the / Artículos: a / an / the

**EN:** Articles answer one question: **does the listener know which one I mean?**

* **a / an** = "one, but you don't know which one yet" — first mention, any one of a group, professions. *I saw **a** dog.* *She is **a** doctor.* Use **an** before a **vowel sound**: *an apple, an hour* (silent h!) — but *a university* (sounds like "yu").
* **the** = "the specific one we both know" — second mention, or unique things. *I saw a dog. **The** dog was black.* *The sun, the moon.*
* **no article** = talking about things **in general**, with plurals and noncount nouns. *I like **dogs**.* ***Coffee** keeps me awake.* ***Love** is important.*

**ES:** Los artículos responden una pregunta: **¿el oyente sabe de cuál hablo?** **a/an** = "uno, todavía no sabes cuál" — primera mención, cualquiera de un grupo, profesiones (*She is **a** doctor* — ⚠️ en español no hay artículo aquí: *Es doctora*). Usa **an** ante **sonido** de vocal: *an apple, an hour*. **the** = "el específico que ambos conocemos". **Sin artículo** = hablar de cosas **en general**, con plurales e incontables.

⚠️ **The general-statement trap / La trampa de las generalizaciones:** Spanish uses *el/la* for general statements; English uses **nothing**:

| Español | ❌ | ✅ |
|---|---|---|
| Me gusta el café. | ~~I like the coffee.~~ (only if specific coffee) | I like **coffee**. |
| El amor es importante. | ~~The love is important.~~ | **Love** is important. |
| Los perros son leales. | ~~The dogs are loyal.~~ (only specific dogs) | **Dogs** are loyal. |

## Part 4 · Common mistakes / Errores comunes

| ❌ | ✅ | Why / Por qué |
|---|---|---|
| I have many homeworks. | I have a lot of **homework**. | noncount — no plural |
| She gave me an advice. | She gave me **some advice / a piece of advice**. | noncount — no *a/an* |
| He is engineer. | He is **an** engineer. | professions take a/an |
| The music is my passion. (general) | **Music** is my passion. | general → no article |
| I need informations. | I need **information**. | noncount |
| Three persons came. | Three **people** came. | irregular plural |
| a hour / an university | **an** hour / **a** university | article follows the SOUND |

## Part 5 · Practice / Práctica

**A. a / an / the / — (nothing):** 1. She is ___ nurse. 2. I love ___ chocolate. 3. We watched ___ movie last night. ___ movie was fantastic. 4. He waited for ___ hour. 5. ___ children learn languages fast. (general)

**B. Fix the noun / Corrige el sustantivo:** 1. I bought three breads. 2. The teacher gave us two homeworks. 3. There are many persons here. 4. Can you give me an information?

**C. much or many?** 1. How ___ money do you have? 2. How ___ friends came? 3. I don't have ___ time.

---

*Remember / Recuerda:* first ask **count or noncount?** — it decides plural, *a/an*, and *much/many*. Then ask **does the listener know which one?** — it decides *a/an*, *the*, or nothing. General statements take NOTHING, even though Spanish uses *el/la*. / Primero pregunta **¿contable o incontable?** — decide el plural, *a/an* y *much/many*. Luego **¿el oyente sabe cuál?** — decide *a/an*, *the* o nada. Las generalizaciones no llevan NADA, aunque el español use *el/la*.
